1. Executive Summary & Business Case

MTN Group Fintech's MoMo lending products (MoMo Kopa, MoMo Credit, and market-specific credit lines) collectively expose MTN to consumer credit risk across millions of borrowers. The current approach to credit risk management is reactive — the system detects a missed payment and initiates a collection process. By the time a customer misses a first payment, they are typically already 60–90 days into a financial distress spiral that, with earlier intervention, could have been arrested.

UC-36 introduces an AI-powered early warning system that analyses 40+ behavioural signals from MoMo transaction data to detect financial distress up to 90 days before the first missed payment. The system continuously scores every active borrower on a distress probability index and, when signals cross predefined thresholds, triggers a 5-level graduated intervention ladder — ranging from a gentle financial wellness nudge at Level 1 through to proactive debt restructuring at Level 4 and write-off prevention protocols at Level 5.

The financial impact is substantial: a 25–40% reduction in default rates across the MoMo credit book translates directly to lower provisioning requirements, reduced debt collection costs, and improved net credit margin. For a credit book of R500M, a 30% default reduction at a 10% default rate represents R15M in annual provision savings. Equally important, early intervention preserves customer relationships and reduces MoMo churn — distressed customers who receive proactive support are significantly more likely to remain active MoMo users than those who enter formal collections.

2. Problem Statement

Consumer financial distress is not a sudden event — it is a slow-motion process that unfolds over weeks and months, leaving behavioural fingerprints in transaction data long before a formal delinquency event occurs. MTN's current credit risk architecture is blind to these signals until they manifest as missed payments.

Problem 1 — Reactive Risk Management Misses the Intervention Window
The optimal window for financial distress intervention is 30–90 days before a first missed payment, when the customer is still psychologically receptive, financially capable of partial repayment, and the debt is not yet entrenched. Current systems only detect distress at Day 1 post-due-date, by which point the customer is already in a defensive psychological posture, partially or fully illiquid, and the cost of collection is significantly higher. UC-36 moves the intervention 90 days earlier.
Problem 2 — MoMo Transaction Data Contains Rich Distress Signals That Are Unutilised
Daily MoMo transaction data captures multiple proven financial distress precursors: declining average account balance week-over-week, increased airtime top-up frequency (borrowing airtime), decreased merchant payment diversity (cutting discretionary spend), MoMo loan top-up requests, declining incoming payment frequency (income reduction), and irregular repayment timing. These signals have high predictive validity for default events but are currently not aggregated, scored, or acted upon by any system in the MoMo stack.
Problem 3 — One-Size Collections Damages Customer Relationships
The current collections process treats all delinquent customers identically — a rigid escalation sequence regardless of distress cause, severity, or customer value. A customer experiencing a temporary income disruption (retrenchment, medical emergency) is treated the same as a habitual non-payer. This approach maximises short-term collection activity but destroys customer relationships, drives churn, and often leaves both parties worse off than a negotiated arrangement would have. The 5-level intervention ladder in UC-36 is designed to match intervention intensity to distress severity and customer circumstances.

4. The 5-Level Intervention Ladder

The core design principle of UC-36 is that financial distress intervention must be proportional to distress severity. An over-intensive early intervention can alarm a customer and damage trust; an under-intensive late intervention arrives too late to be effective. The 5-level ladder is triggered automatically by the distress score crossing defined thresholds.

1
Level 1 — Financial Wellness Nudge (Score 0.30–0.44)
In-app notification: personalised financial wellness tip based on identified spending pattern. Example: "We noticed your balance has been lower this month. Here are 3 ways to stretch your MoMo wallet." No mention of distress or credit. Goal: early positive engagement. No human involvement required. Automated AI-generated message.
2
Level 2 — Proactive Repayment Support (Score 0.45–0.59)
In-app message offering flexible repayment options: "Did you know you can adjust your MoMo Credit repayment date? Here's how." Plus: automated short-term payment holiday offer (7 days, no penalty, no interest capitalisation). Goal: reduce friction and demonstrate support. Triggered without customer needing to call or apply.
3
Level 3 — Financial Counselling Outreach (Score 0.60–0.74)
Proactive contact by MoMo Customer Experience agent trained in financial wellness. Offer of a free 15-minute financial review call. Goal: understand root cause of distress, identify appropriate support. Agent offered script with NCA-compliant language. Customer may also be offered debt consolidation options within MoMo product suite.
4
Level 4 — Hardship Plan & Restructure (Score 0.75–0.89)
Formal credit restructuring offer: extended repayment term, reduced instalment amount, payment holiday of up to 30 days. Offer generated by AI and approved by credit risk team. NCA Section 86 compliant — customer must give informed consent. Debt counsellor referral provided as an option alongside internal restructure offer. All offers logged for NCR audit purposes.
5
Level 5 — Write-Off Prevention Protocol (Score ≥0.90)
Highest intensity intervention before formal collections. Credit risk committee review. Debt counsellor referral (NCR registered) as preferred resolution. Settlement offer (discounted lump sum payoff). If no response within 14 days: transition to formal collections. Goal: maximise recovery while minimising write-off. All actions documented for FICA/NCR regulatory review.

5. User Stories

US-01 · Early Signal Detection
System Detects Distress 90 Days Ahead
"As a credit risk officer, I want the system to flag financially distressed borrowers at least 90 days before they miss a payment so we can intervene early."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Distress score calculated daily for all active borrowers
  • Model achieves AUC >0.78 on 90-day default prediction
  • Recall >70% for customers who ultimately default
  • False positive rate <20% (precision >80% at threshold 0.60)
  • Model validated on 12-month historical holdout set before deployment
US-02 · Level 1 Nudge
Gentle Financial Wellness Notification
"As a MoMo customer showing early distress signals, I want to receive helpful financial tips that feel supportive, not alarming."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Nudge sent within 24 hours of score crossing 0.30 threshold
  • Message personalised to the specific distress signal detected
  • No mention of credit, loans, or distress in the message
  • Customer engagement rate (tap-through) tracked per nudge type
  • Nudge frequency capped at 1 per 7 days per customer
US-03 · Payment Holiday Offer
Automated 7-Day Payment Holiday (Level 2)
"As a financially stressed MoMo Credit customer, I want to be offered a payment holiday before I miss a payment so I can avoid a negative credit mark."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Payment holiday offered automatically at score 0.45 threshold
  • Customer accepts in-app with one tap; no call required
  • Payment holiday applied immediately; next due date extended 7 days
  • No penalty, no interest capitalisation during holiday period
  • Credit bureau not notified of payment holiday (no negative mark)
US-04 · Debt Counsellor Referral
NCR-Registered Debt Counsellor Referral (Level 4/5)
"As a severely distressed customer, I want to be connected with an NCR-registered debt counsellor who can help me restructure all my debt, not just my MoMo loan."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Referral list of NCR-registered debt counsellors available per province
  • Customer can initiate referral contact directly from the MoMo app
  • Referral consent recorded per NCA Section 86 requirements
  • MTN debt temporarily stayed upon confirmed debt review application
  • Referral outcome tracked and fed back to model training
US-05 · Credit Risk Dashboard
Portfolio-Level Distress Risk Dashboard
"As a credit risk manager, I want a real-time dashboard showing the distribution of distress scores across the credit book so I can manage portfolio risk proactively."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Dashboard shows count and % of borrowers in each intervention level
  • Trend charts: 7-day, 30-day rolling distress score distributions
  • Drill-down by market, product type, loan vintage
  • Alert if Level 4/5 population increases >10% week-on-week
  • Export to CSV for credit committee reporting
US-06 · Customer Consent
POPIA-Compliant AI Processing Consent
"As a compliance officer, I want all AI processing of customer transaction data for distress scoring to be covered by explicit, recorded consent."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Distress scoring AI processing disclosed in MoMo Credit T&Cs
  • Existing borrowers receive in-app consent update notification
  • Opt-out from AI scoring removes customer from distress model (not from credit obligation)
  • Consent record immutable, timestamped, auditable
  • POPIA impact assessment completed and DPO-approved before launch
US-07 · Explainable AI
Model Explainability for Credit Decisions
"As a compliance officer, I want to be able to explain why a specific customer was flagged as distressed so we can defend our processes to the NCR."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Top 3 contributing signals displayed per customer in risk officer view
  • SHAP values calculated and stored for each scored customer
  • Explanation available in plain English for agent use in customer conversations
  • No adverse action taken solely on AI score — human review required at Level 4+
  • Model card documented and available for NCR review
US-08 · Intervention Outcome Tracking
Measure Intervention Effectiveness
"As a product manager, I want to measure how effective each intervention level is at preventing default so we can optimise the intervention ladder over time."

  • A/B testing framework: intervention vs control group comparison
  • Default rate by intervention level reported monthly
  • Model retraining pipeline incorporates intervention outcome labels
  • Intervention ROI (provision savings vs intervention cost) calculated quarterly

6.2 Distress Scoring Model

IDRequirementPriorityNotes
FR-05The AI model SHALL output a continuous distress probability score between 0.00 and 1.00 for each active borrower, representing the estimated probability of default within 90 days. Score SHALL be updated daily.MUSTGradient boosting classifier (XGBoost / LightGBM)
FR-06The model SHALL achieve a minimum AUC of 0.75 on a held-out validation set before production deployment. A shadow scoring period of 30 days SHALL run in parallel with the existing collections model before full cutover.MUSTModel validation gate
FR-07The model SHALL generate SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values for each scored customer, identifying the top 3 contributing distress signals. SHAP values SHALL be stored alongside the distress score for explainability and audit purposes.MUSTNCR explainability requirement
FR-08The system SHALL automatically trigger model retraining when: (a) weekly AUC monitoring drops >3pp below baseline, (b) score distribution shift detected (PSI >0.25), or (c) monthly scheduled retraining interval reached. Retrained models require risk team approval before promotion to production.MUSTMLOps governance requirement

IDRequirementPriorityNotes

Explainable AI Requirement — NCR Alignment
The NCR (National Credit Regulator) requires that any automated system influencing credit terms or collections actions must be explainable and auditable. UC-36 addresses this through: (1) SHAP values stored per scored customer providing feature-level explanation, (2) plain-English explanation available to credit officers and agents, (3) human review mandatory for Level 4/5 actions, (4) model card published and available for regulatory inspection. No customer may be adversely impacted solely on the basis of the AI distress score — human judgment is required in the decision loop at all high-stakes intervention points.
