Executive Summary & Business Case

Financial planning tools — budgeting apps, investment simulators, loan affordability calculators — have traditionally been available only to banked, formally employed individuals with stable incomes and access to comprehensive financial data. MTN MoMo's 65 million subscribers transact primarily through a single channel that generates rich, longitudinal financial behaviour data: every send, receive, bill payment, savings contribution, and airtime purchase is timestamped and stored. Yet this data is used today only for transaction history display and fraud detection. UC-34 unlocks this data as the foundation for a living financial model — a digital twin of each customer's financial life.

The digital twin is a continuously updated simulation model built from each customer's MoMo transaction history. It classifies income streams, recurring expenses, variable spending, savings patterns, and financial shock vulnerabilities. On top of this real-time model, a Monte Carlo scenario engine allows customers to run forward-looking "what if" simulations using natural language queries: "What if I lose my job for 3 months?", "Can I afford a ZAR 500/month loan repayment?", "If I save an extra R200 per month, when will I have enough to buy a fridge?" The simulation is computed on-device where possible, preserving privacy while delivering institutional-grade financial modelling to a smartphone-accessible interface.

The commercial case is equally compelling. Customers who engage with their financial twin demonstrate 2–3x higher conversion rates for cross-sold MoMo financial products — MoMo Loans, MoMo Invest, MoMo Insurance — because the digital twin provides the customer with the information they need to make an informed product decision. Rather than a generic loan offer, MoMo can present: "Based on your income pattern and expenses, you can comfortably afford a R3,000 loan with R280 monthly repayments — your digital twin shows a 94% probability of comfortable repayment without financial stress."

Problem Statement

Problem 1 — Informal Economy Workers Make Major Financial Decisions Without Data
MTN MoMo's core user base — informal traders, domestic workers, gig economy participants — face the same fundamental financial decisions as formally employed individuals: can I afford this loan? What happens to my family if I lose income for 3 months? How long until I can afford a new phone? But they make these decisions with no tools, no data, and no professional advice. Decisions made by gut feel lead to over-indebtedness, missed savings opportunities, and financial fragility that makes customers permanently vulnerable to income shocks. MoMo has the data to change this — but does not yet use it to help customers make better decisions.
Problem 2 — Generic Cross-Sell Offers Convert Poorly Because They Lack Personal Relevance
MoMo's current approach to cross-selling financial products (loans, insurance, investment) is segment-level targeting: broad demographic or transaction-frequency segments receive generic offers. Conversion rates are low because the offer cannot answer the customer's fundamental question: "Is this right for me, specifically?" A customer who sees "Get a R5,000 MoMo Loan!" does not know whether they can actually afford it. A customer whose digital twin shows them personalised affordability analysis — "Based on your income and spending, this R3,000 loan at R280/month leaves you with R840 monthly comfort buffer in 85% of simulated scenarios" — makes an informed decision with higher confidence and lower subsequent default risk.
Problem 3 — Financial Wellbeing is Not Measured or Managed at the Individual Level
MTN MoMo measures aggregate customer value but does not track individual customer financial health trajectory. A customer who is progressively deteriorating financially — declining balance trends, increasing expense-to-income ratio, growing reliance on MoMo Loans — receives no proactive intervention until they default. The digital twin provides a continuous financial health score that enables proactive, personalised wellbeing interventions before crisis, reducing credit losses and improving long-term customer lifetime value.

User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

USER QUERY: "What if I lose my income for 3 months?" DIGITAL TWIN RESPONSE: Based on your income and spending patterns from the last 6 months: Monthly income (avg): R4,200 Monthly essential expenses: R3,100 (rent R1,800, food R800, transport R500) Current savings buffer: R2,340 (MoMo savings pocket) SIMULATION RESULT (10,000 scenarios): Month 1: 92% probability of meeting all essential expenses Month 2: 67% probability (savings depleting, urgent action needed) Month 3: 31% probability (high stress scenario) RECOMMENDED ACTION: Adding R400/month to emergency savings now would raise Month 3 survival probability to 74%.
US-34-01
Income Shock Simulation — Natural Language
"As Sipho, a gig economy driver, I want to ask my MoMo app in plain English 'what happens to my finances if I can't work for 3 months?' and get a clear, visual answer based on my actual income and expenses, so that I can prepare an emergency fund before it's too late."
Acceptance Criteria
  • NL query processed and mapped to income shock scenario parameters within 2 seconds
  • Monte Carlo simulation runs 10,000 scenarios based on current financial model
  • Results presented as: probability of meeting essential expenses per month, time until financial distress (savings depleted), recommended emergency fund amount
  • Visualisation: probability timeline chart, current vs recommended savings level
  • One-tap action: "Set up emergency savings" links directly to savings pocket configuration
  • Computation performed on-device; no financial details transmitted to server
US-34-02
Loan Affordability Calculator
"As a MoMo user considering a loan, I want to see exactly whether I can afford the repayments based on my real spending patterns, not a generic affordability check, so that I borrow confidently and avoid default."
Acceptance Criteria
  • When user views a MoMo Loan offer, digital twin affordability assessment presented automatically
  • Assessment shows: monthly repayment amount, projected remaining disposable income, probability of comfortable repayment across 10K scenarios, stress scenarios where repayment becomes difficult
  • Recommendation: "Comfortable", "Manageable with care", or "High risk — consider a smaller loan"
  • Smaller loan alternative suggested if recommended with calculated comfortable amount
  • Assessment based on real transaction history (6-month rolling), not stated income
US-34-03
Investment Projection — NL Scenario
"As a MoMo user, I want to ask 'if I invest R300 per month for 5 years, what will I have?' and see a realistic projection with range of outcomes, so that I can decide whether to start a MoMo Invest account."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Query parsed and mapped to investment projection parameters (amount, duration, product type)
  • Monte Carlo simulation incorporates MoMo Invest historical return distribution with uncertainty
  • Results: median projected value, 25th/75th percentile range, probability of exceeding specific targets
  • Affordability check: can R300/month be consistently saved based on current income/expense pattern?
  • One-tap: "Start investing R300/month" links to MoMo Invest onboarding
US-34-04
Financial Health Dashboard — Personal Overview
"As a MoMo user, I want to open my app and immediately see a clear picture of my financial health — income vs expenses, savings trend, and one key insight about something I could improve — without reading a long report."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Financial health dashboard loads in <1 second (on-device model pre-computed)
  • Shows: 30-day income vs expenses (waterfall), savings trajectory (last 3 months), financial health score (0–100)
  • One personalised insight per session: e.g., "Your food spending increased 23% this month vs your average"
  • Spending categories auto-classified from transaction data (no manual tagging required)
  • Month-over-month trend indicator for each major spending category
US-34-05
Goal Setting — Savings Milestone
"As a MoMo user, I want to set a goal of saving R5,000 for a new fridge and be told realistically how long it will take based on my actual spending patterns, so that I have a concrete and achievable plan."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Goal set via NL query or structured input (target amount, optional deadline)
  • Digital twin calculates realistic savings timeline based on current surplus after essential expenses
  • Sensitivity analysis: "If you reduce eating out by R200/month, you'd reach your goal 6 weeks sooner"
  • Progress bar updated in real-time as transactions occur
  • Weekly check-in notification: goal progress vs plan, with adjustments if spending changes
  • Goal linked to savings pocket — optional automatic roundup toward goal
US-34-06
Privacy Transparency — Data Subject Rights
"As a privacy-conscious MoMo user, I want to see exactly what financial data my digital twin uses, and be able to delete it entirely with one tap, so that I am in control of my personal financial information."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Privacy settings page shows: data inputs used (transaction history months), model type, computation location (on-device)
  • Data deletion: "Delete my digital twin" destroys all model data and scenario history from device and server within 24 hours
  • Deletion does not affect transaction history (separate data owner)
  • Data export available: user can download their financial model in JSON format (POPIA data portability right)
  • POPIA consent record shown with date, scope, and mechanism to withdraw

Data & AI Model Requirements

Transaction Classification Model
Fine-tuned text + amount classifier on MTN MoMo transaction description corpus. Architecture: lightweight BERT variant (DistilBERT) for description classification + rule-based amount/counterparty features. Categories: salary/income, rent, food, transport, airtime, remittance, savings, loan repayment, entertainment, other. Target accuracy: >90% on held-out MoMo test set. On-device via TFLite, <10MB model footprint.
Income Distribution Modelling
Per-user income model accounts for informal economy reality: irregular payment timing, multiple income streams (salary + informal trade + remittances), seasonal variation (December market trader surge, January trough). Model: kernel density estimation on 12-month income history, producing probability distribution over monthly income. Updated monthly with rolling 12-month window.
Monte Carlo Engine Architecture
Native on-device implementation (Swift for iOS, Kotlin for Android). 10,000 simulation draws from income and expense distributions per scenario. Vectorised computation using device GPU/NPU where available. Simulation state: per-draw income sample, per-draw expense sample, per-draw savings delta, cumulative balance trajectory. Output: 10,000 balance trajectories collapsed to percentile bands, probability calculations, plain-language summary generation.
NL Scenario Parser
Lightweight on-device NL model mapping user scenario queries to structured scenario parameters. Domain: financial planning vocabulary in 6 African languages. Output: scenario type, duration, shock amount, target amount. Low-confidence queries (below 0.75) routed to server-side NL API (query text anonymised: personal names/amounts scrubbed before transmission). Confirmation step before simulation always shown to user.

Compliance & Regulatory

FSCA — Automated Financial Advice Risk
The digital twin's scenario outputs — particularly loan affordability recommendations and investment projections — may be classified as "financial advice" under the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services (FAIS) Act in South Africa, requiring a Financial Services Provider (FSP) licence. The critical distinction is between "information" (presenting simulation outputs without recommendation) and "advice" (recommending a specific product or action). Conservative approach: twin presents scenario analysis as "information to help you decide" rather than "we recommend". Specific product recommendations (e.g., "take this loan") must be carefully worded. FSCA pre-clearance recommended before launch in SA.
POPIA — Financial Data Processing
The digital twin processes financial transaction data which is personal information under POPIA. The on-device architecture is the primary compliance mechanism: processing occurs on the customer's own device, minimising third-party access to sensitive financial patterns. However, POPIA obligations still apply because MTN provides the app that facilitates the processing. Required: explicit informed consent for digital twin feature activation, right of access to processed data (fulfilled by on-device data portability export), right of erasure (fulfilled by model deletion), and DPIA given the automated profiling of financial behaviour.
Responsible AI — Scenario Output Framing
Financial simulation outputs carry inherent uncertainty that must be clearly communicated to avoid false confidence. Requirements: all probability outputs must include confidence interval; plain-language disclaimers ("This is a simulation based on your past patterns — actual future finances may differ"); results should empower, not alarm — negative scenarios presented with actionable mitigations; no scenario should present a single deterministic "future" — always present distribution of outcomes. UX review by financial psychologist recommended before launch.

Sprint Plan & Roadmap

Sprint 1 (Weeks 1–3): Financial Model Foundation

Sprint 1 Deliverables
  • Transaction classification model: training data labelling (50K MTN MoMo transactions), DistilBERT fine-tune, TFLite quantisation, on-device inference integration
  • Personal financial model builder: income distribution modelling (KDE), recurring expense detection, savings rate calculation
  • MoMo transaction webhook integration: real-time model update on new transaction (<30s)
  • On-device encrypted model storage (iOS Keychain / Android Keystore)
  • Financial health dashboard v1: income vs expenses waterfall, savings trajectory, health score
  • POPIA consent capture and data portability export (JSON)
  • FSCA legal opinion engagement initiated

Sprint 2 (Weeks 4–6): Monte Carlo Engine & Scenario Interface

Sprint 2 Deliverables
  • Monte Carlo simulation engine: 10K simulations, <3s on Galaxy A52, native iOS Swift + Android Kotlin
  • 10 pre-built scenario templates: income shock, loan affordability, investment projection, savings goal, rent increase, school fees, major purchase, emergency fund, medical expense, retirement
  • NL scenario parser: on-device lightweight model for 6 languages, anonymised server fallback
  • Simulation results visualiser: probability band charts, plain-language summary, actionable next steps
  • Loan affordability integration: digital twin affordability assessment shown on MoMo Loans offer screen
  • Goal tracker: savings milestone setting, progress bar, weekly check-in notification
  • Investment projection: MoMo Invest product integration, historical return distribution

Sprint 3 (Weeks 7–9): Pilot, Privacy Audit & Cross-Sell Integration

Sprint 3 Deliverables
  • Pilot deployment: MTN South Africa (25,000 user cohort, opt-in), MTN Nigeria (10,000 user cohort)
  • FSCA legal opinion received; advice framing adjusted if required
  • DPIA completed and filed with Group DPO
  • Third-party privacy audit: confirm no financial data egress from device
  • A/B test: digital twin loan offer conversion vs standard offer (60-day test)
  • Transaction classification accuracy measurement on production data (vs 90% target)
  • Scenario engine performance benchmark on top-10 Android devices in SA/Nigeria markets
  • Financial health score validation: domain expert review of scoring methodology
  • User research: scenario usage patterns, comprehension of probability outputs, feature requests
