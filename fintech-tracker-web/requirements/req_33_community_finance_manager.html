Executive Summary & Business Case

Rotating savings groups are among the oldest and most culturally embedded financial institutions in Africa. Known as Stokvels in South Africa, Chamas in Kenya, Esusu or Ajo in Nigeria, Tontines in West Africa, and Susu in Ghana, these rotating community credit associations (ROSCAs) mobilise an estimated R50 billion annually in South Africa alone, with the pan-African total estimated at $50–100 billion. They operate entirely in cash and informally, managed through personal trust, physical meetings, and handwritten ledgers.

The informal, cash-based nature of these groups creates three critical problems: non-payment risk (members who default disrupt the entire rotation), opacity (the group treasurer holds unchecked authority over group funds), and security (large cash payouts create robbery risk). MTN MoMo, as the dominant mobile money platform across these markets, is uniquely positioned to digitise and formalise ROSCA operations — making them safer, more transparent, and more accountable while preserving the cultural trust and social dynamics that make them effective savings vehicles.

UC-33 builds an AI-powered ROSCA management platform on top of the MoMo API, accessible via WhatsApp, USSD, and the MoMo app. Group creation, member onboarding, contribution reminders, rotating payout execution, and dispute resolution are all managed by the AI. Critically, the platform is designed with cultural rule customisation — the immense variation in ROSCA rules across SA (grocery stokvels, investment stokvels, burial societies), Kenya (chama investment structures), and Nigeria (competitive bidding esusu) is accommodated through a configurable rule engine rather than a one-size-fits-all model.

Problem Statement

Problem 1 — Cash-Based ROSCAs Are Vulnerable and Exclusionary
Traditional stokvel/chama operations require physical cash, physical meetings, and physical presence for payouts. This creates vulnerability to theft and robbery (particularly on payout day), exclusion of members who have moved to a different city or country, and dependency on a trusted treasurer who becomes a single point of failure for the group's finances. When a treasurer defaults, steals, or is incapacitated, the entire group's savings are at risk with no formal recourse mechanism.
Problem 2 — Digital Money Platforms Ignore the ROSCA Market
Despite the enormous scale of ROSCA activity across MTN's markets, no mainstream mobile money platform has built a product that genuinely accommodates the social, cultural, and operational realities of stokvel/chama/esusu groups. Existing "savings group" features are generic and fail to accommodate the specific rotation mechanics, contribution timing rules, penalty structures, and cultural decision-making norms that define each community's specific ROSCA variant. This represents a significant unaddressed market need that UC-33 is positioned to fill.
Problem 3 — Dispute Resolution in ROSCAs is Informal and Relationship-Damaging
When a member fails to contribute, the group faces a dilemma: invoke informal social pressure (damaging the relationship), postpone the payout (harming the member in rotation), or absorb the shortfall (creating resentment). There is no neutral third-party mechanism. AI-mediated dispute resolution, backed by a transparent ledger, provides a culturally neutral mechanism for handling contribution failures, payout disputes, and member departures without damaging personal relationships within the group.

User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

US-33-01
Group Creation — Stokvel Founder
"As Mama Thandi, a stokvel founder in Soweto, I want to create our 12-member grocery stokvel on MoMo in under 10 minutes and invite all members via WhatsApp, so that we can stop handling cash at our monthly meetings."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Group creation wizard: group name, type (grocery/investment/burial society), member count, contribution amount, frequency, rotation order
  • Invitation sent via WhatsApp link to each member's number — non-MoMo members prompted to register
  • Group creation completable in under 10 minutes including first member invitation
  • Group creator assigned "admin" role; additional co-admins configurable
  • Group rules stored as digital record, accessible to all members at any time
  • Cultural rule preset: SA grocery stokvel template pre-loaded with sensible defaults
US-33-02
Automated Contribution Reminder & Collection
"As a group member, I want to receive a WhatsApp reminder 3 days before my contribution is due and be able to pay directly from the reminder message, so that I never miss a contribution and let the group down."
Acceptance Criteria
  • AI sends WhatsApp reminder at T-7 days and T-3 days before contribution due date
  • Reminder includes contribution amount, due date, and one-tap payment link
  • Payment link executes MoMo transfer from member wallet to group pool wallet
  • USSD fallback: member can pay via *165*STOKVEL# without WhatsApp
  • Group admin and all members see real-time contribution status on group dashboard
  • Late payment automated follow-up: 24h overdue notification to member and admin
US-33-03
Rotating Payout Execution
"As Nkechi, the member receiving this month's payout, I want the full group contribution pool to be sent automatically to my MoMo wallet on the payout date without anyone having to manually collect cash, so that I receive my money safely and on time."
Acceptance Criteria
  • AI calculates payout amount based on confirmed contributions and group rules (penalty deductions if applicable)
  • Payout executed automatically on configured date to recipient's MoMo wallet
  • All group members receive payout confirmation notification with amount and recipient
  • If outstanding contributions exist, payout is reduced by configured penalty/shortfall rule (configurable per group)
  • Payout history stored in immutable group ledger accessible to all members
  • Next rotation position displayed to all members after each payout
US-33-04
AI Dispute Resolution
"As a group admin, when a member disputes a missed contribution claim, I want the AI to present the objective contribution record and propose a resolution, so that we can resolve the dispute without it becoming personal and damaging friendships."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Dispute raised via WhatsApp or app; AI presents verified contribution record to all parties
  • AI proposes resolution options based on group rules (penalty payment, postponed payout, position swap)
  • Group admin votes to accept proposed resolution; majority voting rules configurable
  • Unresolved disputes after 7 days escalated to MTN customer service for human arbitration
  • Dispute outcome recorded in group ledger with all member visibility
  • Resolution statistics shared with admin for transparency
US-33-05
Chama Investment Track — Kenya
"As a Nairobi Chama treasurer, I want to manage our chama's investment pool separately from the rotating payout pool, with AI tracking returns and member equity stakes, so that we can run a more sophisticated savings club."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Chama template includes: rotating payout pool (ROSCA) + investment pool (separate wallet)
  • Investment contributions tracked with per-member equity percentage
  • Integration with MoMo Invest for pooled investment product access
  • Monthly investment return report distributed to all members via WhatsApp
  • Member equity redemption process supported with AI-guided valuation
US-33-06
Multi-Currency Cross-Border Group
"As a Nigerian diaspora member living in South Africa, I want to participate in our family esusu group across borders with automatic currency conversion, so that distance doesn't exclude me from our family savings tradition."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Group supports members in multiple countries with different MoMo currency wallets
  • Contributions in local currency converted at MoMo FX rate at time of contribution
  • Payout issued in recipient's local currency at prevailing FX rate on payout date
  • FX rate shown to member before contribution confirmation
  • Cross-border transfer compliance: KYC verified members only for cross-border groups
US-33-07
Cultural Rule Customisation — West Africa Tontine
"As a tontine organiser in Ivory Coast, I want to configure competitive bidding rules where the rotation order is determined by who bids the highest interest to the group each cycle, so that our local tontine traditions are respected."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Advanced rule configuration supports: fixed rotation, random rotation, bid-based rotation, admin-assigned rotation
  • Bid-based rotation: members submit sealed bids; highest bid (interest to group pool) wins that cycle's payout
  • Bid management via WhatsApp or app interface
  • Rule set saved as group template; shareable with other groups in same community
  • Market-specific rule templates pre-built for SA stokvel, Kenya chama, Nigeria esusu, West Africa tontine

Data & AI Model Requirements

AI Contribution Reminder Personalisation
Behavioural model per member: optimal reminder timing (when the member historically responds fastest), preferred channel (WhatsApp vs SMS), payment device (smartphone vs feature phone). Reminder timing personalised based on member payment history to maximise on-time contribution rate. No cross-member data sharing — each member's model built from their own interaction history.
AI Dispute Mediator
Rules-based + NL approach: objective ledger data presented first (contribution records, timestamps, amounts). NL generation produces neutral, culturally appropriate dispute summary in member's language. Resolution options generated from group rule set. Escalation classifier identifies disputes likely to require human arbitration (e.g., theft allegations, admin misconduct) and routes immediately to MTN customer service.
Non-Payment Prediction
Member-level risk model predicting likelihood of non-payment for upcoming contribution based on: payment history (on-time rate, average days late), MoMo wallet activity (balance trends, transaction frequency), contribution size relative to typical wallet balance. High-risk members receive earlier reminders and optional partial payment options. Model retrained monthly per group.

Ethnographic Research Requirements (Pre-Development)

Next Step: Ethnographic Research Programme
Before development begins, MTN must commission ethnographic research across South Africa, Kenya, and Nigeria to map the full variation in stokvel/chama/esusu rule sets, social dynamics, dispute resolution customs, and cultural norms around money in group settings. Research questions: What are the boundaries of acceptable AI mediation? What decisions must remain human? How do groups handle death of a member, pregnancy leave, or financial emergency? What are the taboos around group money management? This research directly informs the cultural rule template library and dispute mediation protocols. Estimated: 3-month programme, 50 focus groups across 3 markets.

Compliance & Regulatory

Collective Investment Scheme Risk (South Africa)
Investment stokvels that pool member funds for collective investment may be classified as Collective Investment Schemes (CIS) under the Collective Investment Schemes Control Act (CISCA), requiring registration with the FSCA. MTN must obtain legal opinion on whether the platform constitutes CIS management or merely provides payment infrastructure for self-directed groups. Recommended approach: platform provides payment rails and record-keeping only; investment decisions remain entirely with group members; no platform recommendation of investment products.
Trust Account Obligations
Group pool wallets holding member contributions in trust must comply with trust account regulations in each market. In South Africa, holding client funds in trust triggers obligations under the Financial Sector Regulation Act. The trust account model must be reviewed by Group Legal to ensure MTN MoMo's e-money licence covers the group pooling model. Alternative structure: each member pays directly to the payout recipient on payout date, bypassing pooling entirely.
AML / KYC for Cross-Border Groups
Cross-border ROSCA groups with members in multiple countries trigger cross-border payment compliance: FICA (SA), EFCC regulations (Nigeria), CBK foreign exchange rules (Kenya). KYC verification is mandatory for all members of cross-border groups. Transaction monitoring must flag unusual patterns (e.g., rapid succession of large cross-border contributions). The FIC Act (SA) requires reporting of cross-border transactions above ZAR 10,000 equivalent.

Per-Member POPIA Consent Requirements

  • Each group member must provide individual POPIA consent for: processing of their contribution data, sharing of their payment status with other group members, AI reminder processing, dispute mediation using their transaction records
  • Group admin has additional consent scope: access to all member contribution records for admin purposes
  • Member withdrawal from group must trigger right-to-erasure process for their personal data while preserving the group ledger integrity (contribution amounts without personal identifiers may be retained)
  • WhatsApp-based interactions subject to Meta's data processing terms; members must be informed of WhatsApp data sharing in addition to MTN POPIA consent
