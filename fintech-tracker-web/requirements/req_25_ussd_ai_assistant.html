1. Executive Summary & Business Case

MTN Group Fintech operates Mobile Money (MoMo) services across 13 African markets with over 65 million active subscribers. The predominant access channel is USSD — a protocol available on every GSM handset regardless of internet connectivity or smartphone capability. Despite heavy investment in the mobile app and web channels, more than 70% of Mobile Money transactions in sub-Saharan Africa originate from feature phones over USSD.

The current USSD implementation presents customers with rigid numeric menu trees requiring 6–12 keypresses to execute a simple money transfer. Menu depth, language barriers and cognitive overhead drive an estimated 31% session abandonment rate before transaction completion. Customers who cannot navigate the menus are effectively excluded from the Mobile Money ecosystem despite having a registered account.

UC-25 introduces an AI-powered natural language intent layer sitting between the USSD gateway and the Mobile Money API. Customers can bypass menu navigation by typing plain-text commands — “send 200 to john”, “check balance”, “last 3 txns”, “help me reset my PIN” — which are classified, entity-extracted and executed without requiring the customer to learn menu structure. The system falls back gracefully to structured menus when intent confidence is below threshold, preserving the existing experience as a safety net.

The business case is compelling: a 15-percentage-point reduction in abandonment translates to approximately 9.75 million additional completed transactions per month at current volumes. Customer satisfaction on the USSD channel is projected to improve from NPS +12 to NPS +34 within 12 months of rollout. The solution is built as a per-OpCo gateway adapter pattern, enabling each of the 13 markets to onboard independently without a monolithic platform migration.

2. Problem Statement

The USSD channel, while universal across all 13 MTN markets, is trapped in a 1990s interaction paradigm. Three compounding problems drive the gap this use case addresses:

Problem 1 — Rigid Menu Trees Create Exclusion
Current USSD flows require customers to memorise or navigate hierarchical numeric menus. A customer sending money must: (1) dial *165#, (2) press 1 for MoMo, (3) press 1 for Send Money, (4) enter recipient number, (5) enter amount, (6) enter PIN, (7) confirm. Any deviation — entering a name instead of a MSISDN, skipping a step, timing out — terminates the session. Customers on shared phones, first-time users, and those with low literacy struggle disproportionately, creating a structural exclusion effect in the markets MTN most needs to serve.
Problem 2 — High Abandonment Wastes Revenue and Erodes Trust
An internal analysis of USSD gateway logs across MTN South Africa, Ghana, Uganda and Nigeria identified a 31% weighted-average session abandonment rate before transaction completion. The top abandonment causes are: incorrect menu navigation (38%), session timeout due to menu confusion (29%), unsupported recipient format (16%), language mismatch (11%), and other (6%). Each abandoned session represents a failed revenue opportunity and a customer whose trust in Mobile Money diminishes.
Problem 3 — AI Benefits are Inaccessible to Feature-Phone Users
MTN's AI investment has been concentrated in smartphone app features, web chat and contact-centre tools (ARIA, UC18). The 70% of MoMo users who transact exclusively via USSD on feature phones have received no benefit from this investment. UC-25 closes this equity gap by bringing AI-powered natural language understanding to the most constrained channel, ensuring that the customers most dependent on Mobile Money are not left behind.

3. Goals & Non-Goals

Goals

G-01 Natural Language USSD Transactions
Enable customers to initiate MoMo transactions using plain-text commands in any supported language, without navigating menu trees.
G-02 Sub-3-Second End-to-End Latency
Classify intent, extract entities, validate and return a formatted USSD response within 3 seconds including MoMo API call.
G-03 Reduce Session Abandonment by 15pp
Target abandonment reduction from 31% to 16% within 6 months of full market rollout.
G-04 Multi-Language Support
Support English, French, Swahili, Hausa, Zulu and Amharic at minimum with extensible language adapter pattern.
G-05 Graceful Fallback to Menu
When intent confidence is below threshold, present the customer with the standard USSD menu — no degradation of existing experience.
G-06 Per-OpCo Adapter Pattern
Each of the 13 markets can be onboarded independently with market-specific USSD gateway integration, language packs and compliance rules.

Non-Goals

NG-01 Not a Smartphone App Feature
UC-25 is exclusively for the USSD channel. Smartphone natural language features are covered by ARIA (UC18) and the MoMo app team.
NG-02 Not a Full Chatbot / LLM Deployment
This is an intent classification and entity extraction layer, not a general-purpose conversational AI. Open-ended conversation is out of scope.
NG-03 Not a Replacement for PIN Authentication
All transactions still require PIN entry. UC-25 does not change authentication flows or bypass security controls.
NG-04 Not a Voice Channel
IVR and voice-based interaction is handled by ARIA (UC18). UC-25 addresses text-only USSD input.
NG-05 Not a Fraud Engine
Fraud pre-screening calls PayGuard (UC16) as an integration. UC-25 does not build or own fraud detection logic.
NG-06 Not Internet-Dependent
The AI classification engine is deployed server-side at the USSD gateway. No internet connectivity is required on the customer device.

5. Functional Requirements (FR-01 — FR-25)

5.1 Natural Language Intent Classification

IDRequirementPriorityNotes
FR-01 The system SHALL classify customer free-text USSD input into one of the defined intent categories: SEND_MONEY, REQUEST_MONEY, CHECK_BALANCE, TRANSACTION_HISTORY, PIN_RESET, SUPPORT_ESCALATION, PRODUCT_ENQUIRY, OPT_OUT, and UNKNOWN. MUST UNKNOWN triggers fallback to menu
FR-02 The system SHALL attach a confidence score (0.00–1.00) to each classified intent. When confidence < 0.70, the system SHALL fall back to the standard structured USSD menu for that intent category. MUST Threshold configurable per OpCo
FR-03 The system SHALL support multi-intent detection, identifying a primary intent and up to one secondary intent per message (e.g. "send 200 to john and check my balance" resolves SEND_MONEY primary, CHECK_BALANCE secondary). SHOULD Phase 2 feature
FR-04 The system SHALL log the raw input text, classified intent, confidence score, language detected, and timestamp for every classification event. Logs SHALL be retained per market data localisation requirements. MUST Required for compliance audit

5.2 Entity Extraction

IDRequirementPriorityNotes
FR-05 The system SHALL extract the following entity types from customer input: AMOUNT (numeric value + optional currency), RECIPIENT (MSISDN, registered nickname, or contact name), DATE_RANGE (relative: "last 3", "this week", "yesterday"), ACCOUNT_TYPE (wallet, savings, airtime), and PIN (masked, never logged). MUST PIN must never persist in any log
FR-06 The system SHALL resolve RECIPIENT entities against: (a) the customer's saved MoMo contact list, (b) recently transacted MSISDNs within the last 90 days, (c) UC24 Customer Intelligence contact graph. Resolution SHALL be attempted in that order. MUST Integrates with UC24
FR-07 When RECIPIENT resolves to more than one match, the system SHALL present a disambiguation prompt listing up to 3 candidates within the 160-character limit, prompting the customer to select by number. MUST e.g. "Which John? 1.John K 2.John M 3.Other"
FR-08 AMOUNT entities SHALL be validated against the customer's current wallet balance (for SEND_MONEY intents) and the market-specific transaction limits defined in the MoMo API before presenting a confirmation prompt. MUST Per-market limits from MoMo API

5.4 USSD Protocol Compliance — Response Formatting & Pagination

IDRequirementPriorityNotes
FR-12 Every USSD response message generated by the system SHALL be ≤160 characters including pagination indicators. The system SHALL automatically truncate and paginate content that exceeds this limit using the format "(1/3)", "(2/3)" etc. appended to each page. MUST Hard GSM constraint
FR-13 Paginated responses SHALL support navigation via "0" (next page) and "00" (previous page) commands, consistent with MTN USSD UX standards. MUST Aligns with existing USSD UX norms
FR-14 Transaction history responses SHALL be paginated with a maximum of 3 transactions per page, each formatted as: "DD/MM RECIP R[amount] [status]" within the character budget. MUST Example: "01/05 John K R200 OK"

5.5 Session State Management

IDRequirementPriorityNotes
FR-15 The system SHALL maintain session state keyed on the USSD session ID provided by the gateway, persisting: detected language, classified intent, extracted entities, disambiguation state, pagination offset, and transaction draft for the duration of the USSD session (max 180 seconds). MUST In-memory session store (Redis)
FR-16 On USSD session timeout (180 seconds of inactivity or gateway-terminated session), the system SHALL purge all session state for that session ID within 5 seconds and SHALL NOT persist any partial transaction data. MUST Security and compliance requirement
FR-17 The system SHALL detect when a customer resumes a new USSD session after an abandoned incomplete transaction and MAY offer a context-aware prompt ("Your last session was a R200 send to John. Continue? 1.Yes 2.No") if the prior session ended within 10 minutes. NICE Requires short-lived cross-session store

5.6 Fallback, Consent, Opt-In / Opt-Out

IDRequirementPriorityNotes
FR-18 First-time AI-channel users SHALL be presented with a one-time consent opt-in prompt before any AI processing of their text input: "MTN AI assists your MoMo session. Your text is processed by AI. Press 1 to continue or 2 for standard menu." MUST POPIA / NDPR / Kenya DPA requirement
FR-19 Customers SHALL be able to opt out of AI processing at any time by sending "STOP AI" or equivalent in supported languages ("ARRÊT IA", "SIMAMA AI"). Opt-out SHALL take effect immediately and route the customer to the standard USSD menu for all subsequent sessions. MUST Opt-out state stored in MoMo profile
FR-20 When intent classification confidence < configured threshold OR classified intent is UNKNOWN, the system SHALL silently route to the standard USSD menu tree for the relevant transaction type without informing the customer that AI processing failed. MUST Preserves seamless UX on fallback

5.7 Transaction Confirmation & Fraud Pre-Screen

IDRequirementPriorityNotes
FR-21 Before executing any SEND_MONEY transaction, the system SHALL present a confirmation prompt summarising recipient, amount and fees: "Send R200 to John K (+27821234567)? Fee: R2.00. 1.Confirm+PIN 2.Cancel". Total prompt SHALL be ≤160 chars. MUST Regulatory requirement in all markets
FR-22 Every SEND_MONEY and REQUEST_MONEY transaction SHALL be pre-screened by PayGuard (UC16) before the confirmation prompt is displayed. If PayGuard returns BLOCK or REVIEW, the transaction SHALL be declined with a generic message and the incident logged. MUST Integrates with UC16 PayGuard
FR-23 The system SHALL integrate with UC24 Customer Intelligence to retrieve personalisation signals: frequently sent-to contacts, typical transaction amounts, preferred language. These signals SHALL inform entity disambiguation ordering and response tone. SHOULD Integrates with UC24

5.8 Per-OpCo Gateway Adapter

IDRequirementPriorityNotes
FR-24 The system SHALL expose a standardised USSD Gateway Adapter Interface (UGAI) implemented per OpCo, abstracting differences in USSD gateway vendors (Openwave, Mavenir, Comviva) from the AI classification engine. MUST Currently 4 different gateway vendors across 13 markets
FR-25 Each OpCo adapter SHALL support independent configuration of: confidence threshold, supported languages, transaction limits, USSD short code mapping, fallback menu USSD code, and compliance rule set (POPIA vs NDPR vs Kenya DPA etc.). MUST Config stored in OpCo config service

9. Integration Requirements

System Architecture — AI Intent Layer Stack

USSD Layer
Customer Feature Phone → GSM Network → USSD Gateway (Openwave / Mavenir / Comviva, per OpCo) → USSD Gateway Adapter Interface (UGAI)
AI Intent Layer
Language Detector → Intent Classifier (mBERT-based, quantised) → Entity Extractor (NER) → Response Formatter (160-char enforcer + paginator) → Session Store (Redis, per-market)
Resolution & Enrichment
Contact Resolver → UC24 Customer Intelligence API (personalisation, contact graph) → MoMo Contact List API
Transaction & Safety
UC16 PayGuard API (fraud pre-screen) → MoMo API (balance, transfer, history, PIN reset, fees) → Audit Logger (Kafka, async)
Config & Ops

PIN Data — Zero-Logging Guarantee
PIN values entered during USSD sessions SHALL be redacted at the USSD gateway adapter layer (UGAI) before any data reaches the AI classification engine. The AI layer is architecturally incapable of receiving PIN values. This control is verified by an automated integration test that injects a PIN-like value and asserts it does not appear in any log, session store, or downstream API call payload. This test runs on every deployment pipeline execution.
Consent Audit Trail
Every opt-in and opt-out event SHALL be recorded with: customer MSISDN (hashed), OpCo identifier, action (OPT_IN / OPT_OUT), timestamp (UTC), USSD session ID, and language of consent prompt. This record is immutable once written and retained for the period specified by each market's data protection legislation (minimum 3 years).

11. Out of Scope

Smartphone App Features
Natural language features within the MoMo mobile app are outside UC-25. The app team owns in-app NLP and chat features.
Voice / IVR Interaction
Voice-based customer interaction on USSD or IVR is handled by ARIA (UC18). UC-25 is text input only.
General Purpose Conversational AI
Open-ended Q&A, creative tasks, or interactions outside defined intent categories are not supported. Unrecognised intents fall back to menu.
Fraud Detection Logic
Fraud detection rules and model training belong to UC16 PayGuard. UC-25 consumes PayGuard decisions via API only.
Internet-Connected Channels
WhatsApp, web chat, or app-based Mobile Money channels are out of scope. UC-25 is exclusively the USSD protocol channel.
KYC / Onboarding Flows
Customer registration, ID verification and KYC collection via USSD remain owned by the MoMo Onboarding team.
Agent / Merchant Flows
MoMo agent cash-in/cash-out and merchant payment flows use separate USSD codes not in scope for UC-25 Phase 1.
On-Device AI Model Deployment
The AI classification runs server-side at the gateway. No on-device AI model is deployed to customer handsets.

14. Risks & Mitigations

IDRiskLikelihoodImpactMitigation
R-01 USSD gateway vendors do not support HTTP callback within session latency budget Medium High Gateway capability assessment in discovery phase. SS7 proxy alternative identified for non-HTTP gateways. Gateway upgrade cost included in business case.
R-02 Intent classification accuracy below 90% for low-resource languages (Hausa, Amharic, Zulu) Medium Medium Commission linguistic data collection early. Use transfer learning from mBERT. Set market-specific confidence thresholds — lower threshold markets fall back to menu more often, preventing misclassification errors.
R-03 Regulatory approval delayed in one or more markets, blocking go-live Medium High Engage compliance and OpCo legal in discovery phase. Prioritise markets with clearest regulatory path (SA, Ghana) for Phase 1. Per-OpCo adapter enables market-by-market go-live.
R-04 End-to-end latency exceeds 3 seconds under peak USSD load (250k concurrent sessions) Medium High Quantised on-premise NLP model eliminates external network latency. Cache MoMo fee tables. Pre-warm contact resolution at session start. Load test to 300k concurrent sessions before launch.
R-05 Customer inputs sensitive data (PINs, ID numbers) in free-text commands; AI layer captures inadvertently Low Very High NFR-12 zero-PIN logging enforced at gateway adapter. Automated PII scrubber on all logged text. Pattern-match redaction for MSISDN, ID numbers, card numbers before logging. Pen-test pre-launch.
R-06 Adversarial input crafted to manipulate intent classification and execute unauthorised transactions Low Very High PIN authentication required for all financial transactions regardless of AI classification. PayGuard pre-screen catches anomalous patterns. Rate limiting on USSD session attempts per MSISDN. Red-team exercise pre-launch.
R-07 Model drift post-launch degrades classification accuracy over time Medium Medium NFR-14 weekly automated evaluation pipeline. Alert at 2pp accuracy drop. Monthly model retraining on production sample (with consent and anonymisation). Human review queue for low-confidence classifications.
R-08 Opt-in rate below 60% — customers distrust AI processing of USSD messages Medium Medium Consent language tested with focus groups per market. Emphasise speed and convenience benefit. Easy opt-out at any time. Track opt-in rates per OpCo and iterate consent prompt copy.
