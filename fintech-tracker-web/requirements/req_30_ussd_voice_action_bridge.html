Executive Summary & Business Case

Sub-Saharan Africa is home to an estimated 250 million functionally illiterate adults — individuals who cannot reliably read or type text, yet who are active economic participants with mobile phones. These customers are systematically excluded from Mobile Money services that require text-based interaction, whether through USSD menu navigation or the AI natural language layer introduced in UC-25. Even with simplified menus and intent classification, the irreducible requirement to type text creates an insurmountable barrier for this population.

UC-30 eliminates this barrier entirely. By bridging the voice channel to the USSD action layer, a customer dials a short code, speaks their intent in their mother tongue — one of 15 supported African languages — and the AI executes the Mobile Money action on their behalf without any typing required. The solution extends MTN's ARIA voice engine (UC-18) with 15-language speech-to-text capability and connects to the UC-25 USSD AI intent layer as its execution backbone.

The business case is compelling from both a financial inclusion mandate and a commercial growth perspective. MTN's licence obligations across multiple markets include commitments to financial inclusion that UC-30 directly fulfils. Commercially, reaching the 250M+ adults who are currently excluded represents an untapped subscriber base that competitors who rely on text-only interfaces cannot serve. Voice biometric caller verification built into the flow eliminates the need for PIN entry, providing a superior authentication experience for users who may also struggle with numeric PIN recall.

UC-30 builds on rather than replaces existing infrastructure: UC-18 ARIA provides the voice engine foundation, UC-25 provides the intent execution layer, and the MoMo API provides the transactional backbone. This approach minimises net-new development while maximising reuse across the AI portfolio.

Problem Statement

Despite significant AI investment in the MoMo channel, MTN's digital financial services remain inaccessible to a substantial portion of the population it is mandated to serve. Three interconnected problems drive this gap:

Problem 1 — The Literacy Wall Excludes 250M+ Adults
An estimated 250 million adults across MTN's operating markets are functionally illiterate — they cannot reliably read menu options, type amounts, or respond to text-based prompts. Even UC-25's natural language USSD layer requires the ability to type a sentence. Voice is these customers' only viable digital interaction modality. Current IVR systems are either unavailable in local languages or provide rigid branching trees that mirror the same structural exclusion as text USSD menus.
Problem 2 — Language Fragmentation Creates Service Deserts
Africa's linguistic diversity is extraordinary — with over 2,000 languages across the continent. While MTN's text systems support 6 primary languages (UC-25), voice interaction demands support for far more granular local languages to achieve genuine inclusion. A customer in rural Cameroon speaking Fulfulde, or a trader in northern Ghana speaking Dagbani, has no viable voice interface despite both owning GSM handsets. The voice bridge must reach 15 languages at minimum to close the service desert.
Problem 3 — PIN Authentication Excludes Low-Numeracy Users
Even when customers can initiate a voice interaction, the requirement to key in a 4-digit PIN creates a final exclusion hurdle for users with cognitive barriers to PIN recall, particularly elderly customers and those with low numeracy. Voice biometric verification — passphrase or speaker recognition — provides an authentication mechanism that is both more inclusive and, when implemented correctly, more secure than PIN-based auth for voice channels.

Objectives & Success Metrics

Primary Objectives

OBJ-01 Zero-Literacy MoMo Access
Enable any MTN MoMo subscriber to complete a send money, balance check, or airtime purchase transaction using only voice — no typing, no reading, no PIN entry required.
OBJ-02 15-Language Speech Recognition
Deploy STT capability across 15 African languages with >90% word-error-rate accuracy per language, including low-resource languages such as Fulfulde, Dagbani, Kinyarwanda and Tigrinya.
OBJ-03 Sub-2-Second Speech-to-Intent
Achieve end-to-end latency from end of speech utterance to confirmed intent classification of under 2 seconds at P95, enabling natural conversational pacing over standard GSM voice calls.
OBJ-04 Voice Biometric Authentication
Implement speaker verification as a PIN replacement for voice channel transactions, achieving <1% false acceptance rate and <3% false rejection rate across enrolled users.

User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

US-30-01
Voice-Initiated Money Transfer — Illiterate User
"As Fatou, a market trader in Conakry who cannot read or type, I want to call a number, say 'send 10,000 francs to my sister Mariama' in Pular, and have the money sent, so that I can participate in Mobile Money without needing to read anything."
Acceptance Criteria
  • System recognises Pular speech input and extracts amount (10,000 CFA) and recipient name (Mariama)
  • System resolves 'Mariama' to a registered contact or prompts selection if multiple matches
  • Voice biometric verification confirms caller identity before transaction execution
  • Transaction confirmation is spoken back to caller in Pular before execution
  • Confirmation SMS sent to Fatou's number in Pular (latin script)
  • End-to-end latency from end of utterance to spoken confirmation <4 seconds
US-30-02
Voice Balance Check — First-Time User
"As a first-time voice bridge user, I want to hear my balance in my own language when I call and say 'what is my balance', so that I can manage my money without needing a smartphone or literacy."
Acceptance Criteria
  • System auto-detects spoken language within first 2 seconds of speech
  • Balance is retrieved from MoMo API and spoken back in the detected language
  • First-time callers are guided through a 30-second voice biometric enrolment before balance disclosure
  • Response includes available balance and any pending transactions in natural language
US-30-03
Voice Biometric Enrolment
"As a new UC-30 user, I want to set up my voice as my password in under 2 minutes, so that future calls are secure without needing to remember a PIN."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Enrolment flow prompted on first voice bridge call, completable in under 2 minutes
  • System collects 3 passphrase repetitions and constructs speaker model
  • Model stored on-device (where supported) or encrypted server-side per market data laws
  • Enrolment quality score >0.80 required before model is activated
  • Fallback to numeric PIN available if enrolment quality below threshold
US-30-04
Offline Edge Model — Low-Connectivity Area
"As a user in a rural area with intermittent data coverage but good voice signal, I want the voice bridge to still process my request even when the data network is degraded, so that I'm not excluded by infrastructure limitations."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Edge STT model deployed at telecom base station or MVNO gateway processes speech locally
  • Intent classification for core transactions (send, balance, airtime) available offline at edge
  • MoMo API call queued and executed when connectivity restored, with caller notified by callback
  • Edge model operates within 200ms additional latency vs cloud model
US-30-05
IVR Fallback — Speech Recognition Failure
"As a user whose speech input was not understood, I want to be gracefully guided to a simple button-press IVR menu, so that I can still complete my transaction without being disconnected."
Acceptance Criteria
  • After 2 failed STT attempts, system transitions to IVR menu automatically
  • IVR menu covers top 5 transactions: send money, balance, airtime, last transaction, help
  • Transition is seamless — no call drop, no re-authentication required
  • IVR prompts available in top 3 languages for that market
US-30-06
Language Auto-Detection
"As a customer speaking Kinyarwanda, I want the system to detect my language automatically without me having to select it from a menu, so that I experience a natural, frictionless interaction."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Language detection model identifies language from first utterance with >92% accuracy across 15 supported languages
  • All subsequent prompts and responses delivered in detected language
  • Customer can override by saying "English please" or local equivalent at any point
  • Language preference stored against MSISDN for future sessions
US-30-07
OpCo Product Manager — Language Pack Configuration
"As an OpCo product manager, I want to configure which languages are active in my market and upload custom voice prompts in local dialects, so that the experience is culturally appropriate for my customers."
Acceptance Criteria
  • Admin portal allows per-market language activation/deactivation without code deployment
  • Custom audio prompt upload (WAV/MP3) for each transaction step per language
  • Configuration changes take effect within 60 seconds via hot-reload
  • A/B testing capability for prompt variants with conversion tracking

7.2 Model Architecture

Cloud STT Model
Fine-tuned Whisper Large v3 with per-language LoRA adapters. MoMo financial vocabulary injection (amounts, currency names, transaction types, common recipient names). Real-time streaming inference via WebSocket. Hosted per-market on MTN cloud infrastructure to meet data localisation requirements.
Edge STT Model
Quantised Whisper Medium (INT8) with top-5 language support per market. <500MB footprint for base station deployment. 15-50ms additional latency vs cloud. Covers send money, balance, airtime, last transaction, and help intents in offline mode.
Voice Biometric Model
Speaker embedding model (ECAPA-TDNN or equivalent) producing 192-dimensional voice print. Cosine similarity scoring with market-tuned threshold. Liveness detection to prevent replay attacks. Per-user model updated incrementally with confirmed successful authentications.
Language Identification Model
Lightweight VoxLingua107-derived model fine-tuned on 15 African languages. <10MB footprint, <50ms inference. Runs as pre-processor before STT routing. Confidence threshold: >0.85 for auto-route, below threshold prompts explicit language selection.

Compliance & Regulatory

8.1 Biometric Data Handling

POPIA — South Africa (Biometric Information)
Voice biometric templates constitute "special personal information" under POPIA Section 26. Explicit informed consent required before enrolment. Consent must be recordable, withdrawable, and stored for the duration of the customer relationship. Biometric data must not be transferred outside SA without explicit consent and appropriate safeguards. Data subject access requests must include the right to delete biometric templates.
NDPR — Nigeria
Biometric voice data falls under "sensitive personal data" per NDPR Article 2.1. Specific consent required for biometric processing. Data localisation: voice templates must reside on Nigerian infrastructure. The NITDA must be notified of any biometric processing system before deployment. Annual audit of biometric data processing is required.
Kenya DPA 2019
Biometric data processing requires a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) filed with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) before deployment. Voice enrolment consent must be explicit, documented, and linked to the customer's MoMo account record. Right to erasure must delete the biometric template from all systems within 30 days of request.

8.3 Audio Data Minimisation

  • Raw audio SHALL be processed ephemerally — audio is destroyed immediately after STT conversion
  • STT transcripts retained for maximum 24 hours for QA sampling; purged automatically via TTL
  • No audio recordings used for marketing, profiling or any secondary purpose without explicit consent
  • Transcripts classified as personal data — subject to all applicable data subject rights

Sprint Plan & Roadmap

Sprint 1 (Weeks 1–3): Foundation & STT Core

Sprint 1 Deliverables
  • Extend UC-18 ARIA STT to support 9 languages (English, French, Swahili, Hausa, Zulu, Amharic, Kinyarwanda, Yoruba, Luganda) with fine-tuned Whisper Large v3
  • Language identification model integration (VoxLingua107 fine-tune, 15 languages)
  • Voice bridge ingress layer: SIP/RTP handler, end-of-utterance detection, audio pre-processing (noise cancellation)
  • UC-25 Intent API adapter — route classified voice intent to existing UC-25 execution pipeline
  • IVR DTMF fallback scaffold for 5 core transaction types
  • Per-market audio processing deployment on MTN cloud (SA, Nigeria, Ghana as pilots)
  • Ephemeral audio processing pipeline with 24h transcript TTL

Sprint 2 (Weeks 4–6): Biometrics, Edge & Remaining Languages

Sprint 2 Deliverables
  • Voice biometric enrolment flow: guided prompt sequence, 3-repetition model construction, quality scoring
  • Speaker verification service: ECAPA-TDNN embedding, cosine similarity, liveness detection
  • Encrypted biometric template store with per-market data residency enforcement
  • Edge STT model: quantised Whisper Medium (INT8) for 5-language offline mode
  • Offline transaction queue with async MoMo API execution and caller callback
  • Remaining 6 languages: Twi/Akan, Igbo, Fulfulde, Xhosa, Dagbani, Chichewa (training data procurement concurrent)
  • TTS integration for spoken confirmations per language
  • POPIA / NDPR consent capture flow for biometric enrolment

Sprint 3 (Weeks 7–9): Pilot, Compliance & Hardening

Sprint 3 Deliverables
  • Pilot deployment: MTN South Africa, Nigeria, Ghana with real caller traffic (5,000 user cohort per market)
  • STT accuracy benchmarking per language on production audio samples
  • Voice biometric FAR/FRR measurement on enrolled pilot population
  • Regulatory filing: DPIA for Kenya; POPIA compliance documentation for SA; CBN alignment for Nigeria
  • OpCo admin portal: language pack management, prompt upload, A/B testing configuration
  • Load testing: 50,000 concurrent voice calls sustained 30 minutes
  • Security red team: replay attack testing, voice spoofing resistance, adversarial STT input
  • Full rollout plan for remaining 9 markets with per-market timeline
