Executive Summary & Business Case

Africa has the world's lowest insurance penetration rate: less than 3% of the population holds any form of formal insurance cover, compared to 50–80% in developed markets. The reasons are systemic: traditional insurance products require face-to-face sales agents, complex paper forms, bank accounts for premium payments, and lengthy claims processes. For the 200+ million MTN customers across 13 markets who live below the middle-income threshold, these barriers are insurmountable. Yet these are precisely the people most exposed to financial shocks from illness, crop failure, device loss, and death in the family.

UC-29 delivers parametric and behavioural micro-insurance that can be enrolled, managed, and claimed entirely over USSD on any feature phone, with premiums as low as R5 per month collected via MoMo wallet and claims paid instantly to the MoMo wallet when a parametric trigger fires or a covered event is verified. No paperwork. No branch visit. No medical examination. No bank account required. Four product types cover the highest-impact risks: Crop Insurance (parametric weather triggers), Hospitalisation Cover (daily cash benefit), Device Insurance (phone theft/damage), and Funeral Cover (lump-sum payout).

The commercial model is a partnership with licensed insurance underwriters per market, with MTN acting as the distribution and administration platform earning 15–20% commission on premiums. At 5 million policyholders paying an average R25/month, the gross premium volume reaches R1.5 billion per year, generating R225–R300 million annual commission for MTN with near-zero marginal cost of adding new policyholders.

Problem Statement

Problem 1 — Financial Shocks Wipe Out Years of Savings in a Single Event
Across MTN's markets, the most common cause of households falling into poverty is a single uninsured shock: a death in the family requiring funeral costs of R3,000–R15,000, a hospitalisation costing R1,500–R10,000 out-of-pocket, or a crop failure destroying an entire season's income. Without insurance, families sell assets, borrow at predatory rates, or rely on community fundraising. Micro-insurance at R5–R50/month can break this cycle completely for any individual risk category.
Problem 2 — Traditional Insurance Distribution Is Structurally Inaccessible
Insurance companies in Africa distribute through tied agents earning commission, requiring minimum policy sizes to make economics viable. A R5/month crop insurance policy cannot support a commission-earning agent visit. The result is a market failure: customers most in need of insurance cannot access it. MTN's USSD network eliminates distribution cost entirely — USSD enrolment costs cents per policy vs hundreds of rands per agent-sold policy, making micro-premiums commercially viable.
Problem 3 — Claims Processes Are Opaque and Slow, Destroying Customer Trust
Even when lower-income Africans obtain insurance, claims processes are frequently so opaque, slow, and document-intensive that many valid claims are never paid. Claims repudiation rates in South African micro-insurance products have been reported at 30–40% — disproportionately affecting lower-income claimants lacking documentation. Parametric insurance eliminates this entirely: when the trigger fires (rainfall below threshold, confirmed hospitalisation), the claim pays automatically without the customer filing anything.

User Stories

US-01 · Funeral Cover Enrolment
Customer Enrolls in R5/Month Funeral Cover via USSD
“As a low-income MoMo customer, I want to get funeral cover for my family for R5 per month via USSD with no paperwork or agent visit required.”
Acceptance Criteria
  • Enrolment completes in ≤5 USSD interactions from dial to policy issued
  • Customer selects cover amount and enters beneficiary MSISDN
  • First premium deducted from MoMo wallet at enrolment
  • Policy number and cover summary sent via SMS within 60 seconds
US-02 · Parametric Crop Insurance Claim
Automatic Payout on Rainfall Trigger
“As a farmer with crop insurance, I want my claim to be paid automatically when rainfall falls below the insured threshold, without filing any paperwork.”
Acceptance Criteria
  • Rainfall data checked daily from weather API for insured district
  • When trigger fires: claim created automatically, no customer action needed
  • MoMo payout credited within 24 hours of trigger date
  • SMS: “Your crop insurance paid R[amount] to your MoMo. Ref: CLM-12345.”
US-03 · Hospitalisation Cash Benefit
Daily Cash Benefit Triggered on Hospital Admission
“As a hospitalised MoMo customer, I want to claim a daily cash benefit by simply reporting my admission via USSD from any phone.”
Acceptance Criteria
  • Customer dials USSD, selects “Report hospitalisation”
  • Enters hospital name and admission date
  • Claim sent to underwriter for verification within 2 hours
  • Daily benefit paid to MoMo from day 2 of admission, up to 30 days
US-04 · Policy Management
Customer Views and Manages All Active Policies
“As a policyholder with multiple cover types, I want to see all my active policies and premiums in one USSD screen.”
Acceptance Criteria
  • All active policies listed: type, cover amount, premium, renewal date
  • Option to cancel any policy from the USSD menu
  • Cancellation takes effect end of current month
  • Beneficiary update available via USSD for funeral cover
US-05 · Device Insurance Claim
Report Phone Theft or Damage from Borrowed Phone
“As a device insurance policyholder whose phone was stolen, I want to report the claim from a borrowed phone using my MSISDN and PIN.”
Acceptance Criteria
  • Claim reported via USSD from any phone using MSISDN + PIN authentication
  • Police case number entered for theft claims (within 10 days)
  • Claim assessed by underwriter within 48 hours
  • Approved: device replacement voucher or MoMo equivalent within 72 hours
US-06 · Premium Lapse Warning
Customer Warned Before Policy Lapses
“As a policyholder with a low MoMo balance, I want an SMS warning 3 days before my premium deduction so I can top up in time.”
Acceptance Criteria
  • Balance checked 3 days before each premium due date
  • SMS warning sent if balance insufficient for premium
  • 5-day grace period before policy lapses
  • 4 retry attempts for premium deduction over the grace period
US-07 · FSCA Disclosure at Enrolment
Mandatory Product Disclosure Before First Premium
“As a regulator, I want every new policyholder to see the key policy terms and exclusions before their first premium is deducted.”
Acceptance Criteria
  • Key terms screen: cover amount, exclusions, premium, claim process
  • Customer presses 1 to accept or 2 to decline — no policy issued without acceptance
  • Acceptance decision and timestamp stored immutably
  • Full policy wording sent via SMS URL within 60 seconds of acceptance
US-08 · Underwriter Partnership View
Insurance Partner Monitors Portfolio via Dashboard
“As an insurance underwriter partner, I want a real-time dashboard showing active policies, claims triggered, and loss ratio per product type.”
Acceptance Criteria
  • Underwriter dashboard shows: active policies, premium volume, claims triggered, loss ratio
  • Segmented by product type, market, and time period
  • Data refreshed at minimum hourly
  • Claim approval / rejection workflow integrated into dashboard

Next Step: Identify Insurance Underwriting Partner Per Market
The first and most critical action item for UC-29 is to identify and contract an FSCA-licensed (South Africa) and equivalent-licensed insurance underwriter for each target market. MTN cannot legally sell insurance directly. Potential underwriter partners for South Africa include: Hollard Micro-Insurance, Old Mutual Emerging Markets, Sanlam Sky, Clientele Life. For Ghana: Enterprise Insurance, Prudential Life Ghana. A white-label / MNO distribution partnership model is well-established in African micro-insurance (e.g. Hollard-Tigo in Ghana). Commercial negotiation lead time: 3–6 months per market.
Next Step: Define Parametric Trigger Data Sources
Each parametric product requires a legally agreed trigger data source specified in the policy schedule. For crop insurance, this means: selecting the meteorological data provider, defining the specific weather station or gridded data source for each market zone, specifying the rainfall threshold per crop type and season, and having the trigger definition reviewed and approved by the underwriter and regulator. This work requires agricultural expertise and meteorological data analysis, estimated 6–8 weeks per market for crop insurance parametric design.
