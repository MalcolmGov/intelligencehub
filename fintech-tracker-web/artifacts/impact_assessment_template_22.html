Change Summary

The Information Regulator has proposed a material amendment to Section 72 of POPIA (Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013), which governs the transfer of personal information to third parties in foreign countries. The amendment introduces three new mandatory requirements:

1
Real-Time Consent Verification: All transfers of personal information to third-party processors domiciled outside South Africa must be preceded by a real-time, machine-readable consent confirmation from the data subject. Bulk or standing consents are no longer sufficient for ongoing automated transfers.
2
Transfer Logging and Audit Trail: A complete, tamper-proof audit log of all cross-border transfers must be maintained for a minimum of 5 years and made available to the Information Regulator within 24 hours of request. Logs must include: data subject ID (pseudonymised), data categories transferred, receiving country, recipient identity, and consent reference.
3
Binding Third-Party Agreements: All third-party processor agreements must be updated to include mandatory POPIA-equivalent privacy protections, certified by the responsible party (us) before the first transfer under the new regime.

07Risk If Non-Compliant

Non-Compliance Risk Register

Financial
Regulatory Fine: Section 107 of POPIA provides for fines up to R10 million and/or imprisonment of up to 10 years for the responsible party. The Information Regulator has demonstrated an increasing willingness to pursue enforcement actions since its 2022 enforcement powers activation.
Operational
Suspension of Cross-Border Transfers: The Information Regulator may issue a prohibition notice halting all cross-border data transfers pending compliance. This would immediately impact our international payment processing (estimated 34% of transaction volume) and cloud-hosted systems in Ireland and Germany.
Reputational
Public Enforcement Notice: Enforcement notices are published publicly on the Information Regulator's website. A published enforcement action against the organisation would materially harm customer trust, investor confidence, and our financial services licence standing.
Strategic
Third-Party Processor Risk: Failure to update third-party processor agreements as required by the amendment creates a cascading liability — the organisation remains legally responsible as the responsible party for all data processing performed by non-compliant processors on our behalf.
Licence
FSP Licence Conditions: FSCA has indicated in recent guidance that material POPIA non-compliance may be considered a conduct breach affecting FSP licence conditions. A POPIA enforcement action could trigger a concurrent FSCA investigation.
