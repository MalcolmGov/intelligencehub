MTN Group Fintech — Intelligence System Strategy

Artifact bundle (draft for executive review)
Prepared for: Programme leadership · Scope: ~70M customers, 13 African markets

Executive summary

This document consolidates two strands of guidance: (1) disruptive themes that change how customers interact with MTN Fintech beyond incremental UX improvements, and (2) a deeper treatment of an organisation-wide intelligence system—distinct from generic CRM—that addresses fragmented data, weak single-customer visibility, and siloed systems.

Part A — Eight disruptive strategic themes

1. Financial OS — one intelligent layer across products

Customers juggle airtime, mobile money, savings, loans, and merchant journeys. A disruptive move is one orchestration layer that respects consent and channel (USSD, app, WhatsApp, agent) and coordinates money movement, insurance, bills, and savings—without forcing users to learn internal product boundaries.

  • Interaction shift: From opening each product → stating goals (fees, rent, growth).
  • Commercial angle: Goal-based wallets, smart splits, predictive nudges tied to cash-flow signals (privacy-by-design).

2. Agent + AI copilot for the real economy

Agents are the retail face of digital money. Superhuman tooling: KYC assist, fraud cues, liquidity forecasting, voice-first training in local languages, outcome-aligned commissions.

  • Interaction shift: Agent as trusted advisor, not only cashier.
  • Offers: Agent SaaS bundles, float credit with behaviour limits, micro-insurance at counter.

3. USSD & feature-phone AI that completes tasks

Large volumes remain on USSD/SMS. Disruption is task completion (pay, dispute, save, claim) via adaptive short menus plus voice callback where appropriate.

  • Offers: AI-assisted USSD as a B2B2C platform for governments, insurers, logistics.

4. Embedded finance in everyday journeys

Payments, credit, and insurance embedded in education, health, agriculture, mobility, gig work—not as separate apps.

5. Trust architecture as a product

Transparent dispute handling, explainable holds, privacy-preserving fraud signals, verifiable receipts (merchant + buyer).

6. Cross-border clarity for families and SMEs

Quoted fees and paths, predictable settlement, purpose-tagged flows (education, health), SME trade wallets.

7. Network & data as assets (responsible value exchange)

Opt-in models where clearer consent unlocks fairer pricing or benefits—never covert surveillance.

8. Developer & SME ecosystem

Sandboxes, APIs, settlement, identity hooks—infrastructure for local innovators and embedded finance partners.

Part B — Expanded view: intelligence system vs generic CRM

Why this is not “a CRM”

A CRM optimises how internal teams work (pipeline, cases, campaigns). An intelligence system optimises how the organisation behaves toward each customer (and merchant/agent) under explicit policy and regulation.

DimensionGeneric CRMIntelligence system
Core objectLead / opportunity / ticketResolved identity + timeline + intent + risk + context
Data modelManual fields + integrationsCanonical entities + event spine + relationship graph
OutputsDashboards, listsScores, triggers, next-best-action, explanations, audit trail
Success metricPipeline velocity, SLAOutcome lift + fairness + compliance evidence

Single customer view = three layers

1. Identity resolution. Link MSISDN, wallet, app login, agent journeys, merchant IDs, ID documents where permitted—probabilistic matching, rules, and human review for high-stakes cases.

2. Unified timeline. Append-only event stream: recharge, payments, lending touches, care, USSD/app sessions, agent notes—one chronological truth with source IDs preserved.

3. Interpretation layer. Derived attributes: liquidity stress, churn, scam exposure, eligibility, next likely need, reason codes for recommendations—not only scores.

“Financial OS” in operational terms

  • Ingest events from billing, MoMo, lending, insurance partners, care, and (where allowed) network usage.
  • Resolve identity and build the timeline.
  • Apply policies: credit rules, consent, marketing suppression, vulnerable-customer treatment.
  • Decide next actions: message, limit change, agent prompt, fee path, education—not random batch campaigns.
  • Explain for audit and frontline (“why we show this”).
  • Learn via controlled experiments, not silent model drift.

Integration without Big Bang replacement

  • Event-first spine: domains publish facts (“payment succeeded,” “loan declined—reason X,” “USSD abandoned step 3”) with schemas.
  • Canonical party ID mapping to legacy keys per source.
  • Anti-corruption layer: messy source data stays at source; the intelligence layer translates into canonical events.

Customer-visible disruptive capabilities

  • Goal-aware money: stated goals drive splits, reminders, channel choice, merchant rails.
  • Continuity across channels: USSD ↔ app ↔ agent with role-based masking.
  • Pre-emptive trust: fraud scenarios with clear messaging + agent playbooks.
  • Merchant twin: same architecture for merchants (many people behind one shop).

Internal / strategic capabilities

  • Next-best-action constrained by regulation and fairness.
  • Dynamic behavioural segments vs annual CRM hygiene projects.
  • Unified experimentation with country-level policy wrappers.

Governance (non-negotiable)

  • Purpose limitation and consent per use case.
  • Model risk and fairness reviews for credit and pricing-adjacent decisions.
  • Explainability tiers: deep for regulators/agents; simple for customers.
  • Human oversight for irreversible or sensitive actions.

Phased delivery

  1. Golden journey: one high-value journey end-to-end (e.g. liquidity stress, repeat lending funnel, merchant float)—identity + timeline + two intervention models.
  2. Expand event vocabulary only when each stream improves measurable decisions.
  3. Agent + digital parity: agents must trust the timeline or customers will not.

Executive positioning (one sentence)

Build a decision-and-memory layer so MTN Fintech behaves as one intelligent institution toward each customer—without pretending that CRM alone fixes fragmentation.

Part C — Next decisions (for programme alignment)

  • Select first golden journey and success metrics (revenue, NPS/trust, regulatory narrative).
  • Confirm legal anchors for identity per operating company.
  • Choose MVP posture: orchestration (customer-visible actions) vs analytics-only internal pilot first.
  • Architecture fork: event bus vs lakehouse-first; batch vs real-time; packaged CDP vs custom intelligence layer—drive from constraint list, not vendor defaults.
