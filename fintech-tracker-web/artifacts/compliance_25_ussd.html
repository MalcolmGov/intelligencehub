Scope of this Document

This document covers the USSD AI Assistant component of MTN Group Fintech's Mobile Money platform. The assistant processes customer intents (balance enquiry, send money, buy airtime, loan applications) via USSD short codes, using a Natural Language Understanding (NLU) model to classify intent and extract entities from free-text USSD input.

The system operates across 13 markets: South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Uganda, Ghana, Cameroon, Ivory Coast, Zambia, Rwanda, Benin, Guinea, Congo (DRC), and Eswatini. The four markets with mature data-protection legislation (SA, Nigeria, Kenya, Uganda) are analysed in depth; the remaining nine are covered in the per-market mapping table.

This document does not constitute legal advice. It is a compliance framework to guide implementation. All sections flagged for legal counsel review (Section 15) must be reviewed by qualified in-market counsel before go-live.

Top 5 Compliance Priorities

P1 — USSD Telco Log DPA remediation

Renegotiate data processing agreements with all telco partners to govern retention and deletion of USSD session logs containing financial transaction data.

P2 — Consent ledger implementation

Build a multi-market consent ledger linked to MSISDN with per-jurisdiction consent records, timestamps, and opt-out capability via *XXX*0# command.

P3 — Nigeria data localisation

NDPR requires personal data of Nigerian citizens to be stored on servers physically located in Nigeria. Current architecture uses shared AWS infrastructure; a Nigeria-specific data residency design is required.

P4 — AI explainability documentation

NLU intent classification affecting loan eligibility constitutes automated decision-making under POPIA and NDPR. An explainability framework and human-review escalation path must be documented.

P5 — Information Officer appointments

POPIA requires a designated Information Officer registered with the Information Regulator. Equivalent appointments required in Nigeria (NITDA), Kenya (ODPC), and Uganda (PDPO).

POPIA — South Africa

Deep Dive
Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 Effective 1 July 2021 Regulator: Information Regulator (SA)

Data Minimisation in USSD Logs (Section 10)

POPIA Section 10 — Further Processing Limitation
  • Requirement: Personal information must be adequate, relevant and not excessive for the purpose of processing.
  • USSD session logs inherently capture MSISDN, session timestamp, USSD input strings (which may contain amounts, beneficiary numbers, PINs if mistyped), and intent classification outputs.
  • Control required: Implement real-time log scrubbing to remove full USSD input strings after intent extraction; retain only classified intent + anonymised session token.
  • Telco-side logs: MTN's network infrastructure retains USSD session data independently. Data Processing Agreement must limit telco retention to 30 days and prohibit secondary use.
  • Current status: Gap — no log scrubbing implemented; DPA with telco not executed.

Consent via USSD — Sections 11 and 69

POPIA Section 11 — Lawful Processing; Section 69 — Direct Marketing
  • Lawful basis options: Consent (s11(1)(a)), contractual necessity (s11(1)(b)), legitimate interest (s11(1)(f)) — the USSD AI assistant can rely on contractual necessity for core Mobile Money processing, but AI/NLU analytics require explicit consent.
  • Opt-in mechanism: First-use USSD screen must present a consent notice and require affirmative keypress (e.g., "1 to agree"). Silence or failure to respond does not constitute consent under POPIA.
  • Opt-out: Must be possible via *XXX*0# or by pressing "0" from any menu. Withdrawal must take effect within 3 business days.
  • Direct marketing via USSD push: Section 69 requires an opt-in for electronic direct marketing. AI-generated USSD push campaigns require separate consent collection.
  • Consent record: MSISDN, consent timestamp, consent version, market jurisdiction, channel (USSD), and withdrawal timestamp must be stored in consent ledger.
  • Current status: Partial — consent screen designed but ledger not implemented.

Right to Erasure for Session Data (Section 23)

POPIA Section 23 — Rights of Data Subjects
  • Data subjects may request correction or deletion of their personal information. For USSD AI, this means session logs, intent classification records, user behaviour profiles used for AI training, and consent records.
  • Erasure complexity: If session data has been used to train or fine-tune the NLU model, erasure from training data (model unlearning) is technically complex. A documented approach to model retraining upon erasure requests is required.
  • USSD request channel: Customers must be able to initiate erasure requests via USSD. Suggested command: *XXX*9*3# — opens "Manage My Data" submenu.
  • Response timeline: POPIA does not specify a fixed timeline but "reasonably practicable" — best practice and alignment with GDPR precedent sets 30 days.
  • Retention vs. erasure conflict: FICA requires transaction records retained 5 years; POPIA erasure cannot override statutory retention obligations. A documented conflict-resolution policy is required.

POPIA Sections 8–25 Mapping for USSD AI Use

Conditions for Lawful Processing
  • Section 8 — Accountability: MTN SA as responsible party must ensure sub-processors (telco, cloud vendors, NLU model provider) are contractually bound to POPIA-equivalent standards.
  • Section 9 — Processing Limitation: NLU model must process only data necessary to classify intent; raw USSD strings must not be forwarded to third-party AI APIs.
  • Section 12 — Collection directly from subject: Data is collected directly from the customer via USSD — compliant. Indirect collection (e.g., from telco CDRs for AI training) requires notification.
  • Section 13 — Collection for specific purpose: AI intent classification purpose must be stated in privacy notice. Subsequent use for AI model improvement requires separate consent or anonymisation.
  • Section 14 — Further processing: Using session data to train AI models for new products = further processing; requires compatibility assessment or fresh consent.
  • Section 19 — Security safeguards: Appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures required. For USSD: end-to-end encryption at application layer (USSD layer itself is unencrypted), access controls on session log databases, penetration testing.
  • Section 22 — Breach notification: Information Regulator must be notified as soon as reasonably possible. Data subjects notified if breach likely to result in substantial harm. 72-hour target (aligned with GDPR).
  • Section 23 — Data subject rights: Access, correction, deletion — see erasure section above.
  • Section 25 — Transfers outside SA: Cross-border data transfers (e.g., to shared AWS region) require recipient jurisdiction to have adequate protection or binding contractual clauses equivalent to POPIA Schedule 1.

Information Officer Obligations

POPIA Section 55 — Information Officer
  • MTN SA must register a designated Information Officer with the Information Regulator of South Africa.
  • The Information Officer is responsible for encouraging POPIA compliance, dealing with requests from data subjects, and working with the Regulator.
  • A Deputy Information Officer should be designated for the USSD AI programme specifically, given the novel risk profile.
  • Information Officer must maintain a PAIA Manual updated to reflect USSD AI data processing activities.
  • All third-party operators (telco partners, cloud providers, NLU vendors) must be listed as operators in the Information Officer's processing register.
  • Current status: Compliant — Information Officer registered; PAIA Manual update in progress.

NDPR — Nigeria

Deep Dive
Nigeria Data Protection Regulation 2019 Nigeria Data Protection Bill 2023 (enacted) Status: Gap — data localisation not met

Lawful Basis for AI Processing

NDPR Article 2.2 — Grounds for Processing
  • NDPR requires at least one lawful basis for processing personal data. For the USSD AI Assistant operating in Nigeria, the applicable bases are:
  • Consent (Art 2.2(a)): Required for AI intent classification used beyond core service delivery (e.g., behavioural analytics, marketing personalisation). Must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous.
  • Contractual necessity (Art 2.2(b)): Applies to NLU processing required to execute a Mobile Money transaction — balance check, send money, airtime purchase.
  • Legal obligation (Art 2.2(c)): AML/KYC transaction monitoring by the AI falls under CBN regulatory obligations.
  • Legitimate interests (Art 2.2(f)): Can be relied upon for fraud detection AI models. A Legitimate Interests Assessment (LIA) must be documented and retained.
  • AI-specific concern: Where the NLU model makes decisions that affect access to financial services (loan eligibility, transaction blocking), consent alone is insufficient — the Nigeria DPB 2023 aligns with GDPR Article 22 on automated decision-making restrictions.

Consent Collection via USSD

NDPR Article 2.3 — Consent Standards
  • NDPR requires consent to be a "freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication" — higher standard than implied consent.
  • USSD consent screen design: Must include: (1) identity of data controller, (2) purpose of AI processing, (3) data categories processed, (4) right to withdraw. All within 182-character USSD display limit — condensed version must link to full notice via USSD callback or SMS.
  • Consent must be obtained before AI processing commences — first-use session must present consent notice before any intent classification occurs.
  • Nigeria-specific: consent for children's data (under 13) requires parental consent. Age verification mechanism needed via USSD KYC linking.
  • Withdrawal: Must be as easy as giving consent. *XXX*0# opt-out command required, with confirmation SMS to MSISDN.

Data Localisation Requirements

NDPR Article 2.12 — Transfer to Third Countries
  • Requirement: Personal data of Nigerian citizens must be stored in servers physically located in Nigeria. This is one of the most stringent data localisation requirements on the continent.
  • Current gap: MTN Group Fintech's USSD AI infrastructure uses shared AWS infrastructure across multiple regions. Nigerian customer data must be isolated to AWS Africa (Cape Town) or a Nigeria-based data centre — neither of which is currently configured.
  • NLU model inference: If inference calls are made to a model hosted outside Nigeria, this constitutes a cross-border data transfer and violates localisation requirements unless NITDA approval is obtained.
  • Remediation required: (1) Deploy Nigeria-specific NLU inference endpoint within Nigeria, (2) Implement data residency tagging to prevent Nigeria-origin data from leaving defined boundary, (3) Apply to NITDA for any required cross-border transfer approval.
  • Penalty: NDPR provides for fines up to 2% of annual gross revenue or ₦10 million, whichever is greater. Nigeria DPB 2023 increases penalties significantly.
  • Current status: Gap — critical. Go-live in Nigeria blocked pending remediation.

NITDA Notification and Registration

NDPR Article 4 — Data Controller Obligations
  • Data controllers and processors in Nigeria processing personal data of more than 1,000 data subjects must file an annual data audit with NITDA.
  • MTN Nigeria as a data controller must register all major processing activities including the USSD AI Assistant with NITDA's Data Protection Compliance Organisation (DPCO) framework.
  • A Data Protection Compliance Organisation (DPCO) — a licensed third-party auditor — must conduct the annual audit. NITDA-licensed DPCOs must be engaged.
  • Processing activity for USSD AI: submit a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) to NITDA for the AI decision-making component affecting financial services access.
  • Registration of DPO equivalent (Data Protection Officer) with NITDA required for organisations processing large volumes of sensitive data.
  • Current status: Partial — MTN Nigeria registered with NITDA; DPIA for AI component not yet submitted.

Kenya Data Protection Act 2019

Deep Dive
Data Protection Act No. 24 of 2019 Effective November 2019 Regulator: Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC)

Data Subject Rights via USSD Channel

Kenya DPA Sections 26–34 — Data Subject Rights
  • Right of access (s26): Data subjects can request information about what data is held. Via USSD: *XXX*9*1# to receive an SMS summary of data categories held and processing purposes.
  • Right to rectification (s27): Customers can correct personal information. For USSD AI, this affects NLU user profile data (language preference, frequent payees, spending patterns).
  • Right to deletion (s28): Equivalent to right to erasure — conflicts with CBK transaction record retention (7 years). Policy must document which data can be deleted vs. must be retained for regulatory purposes.
  • Right to object to automated decisions (s34): Any automated decision with legal or significant effect requires the ability to request human review. Loan applications processed by AI must have a human escalation path.
  • Right to data portability (s38): Data must be provided in a structured, commonly used format. For USSD-originated data, CSV export via email (if email on file) or physical branch collection.
  • USSD rights hub: A dedicated USSD submenu (*XXX*9#) for data subject rights is recommended — balance between regulatory compliance and USSD session complexity.

ODPC Registration

Kenya DPA Section 16 — Registration of Data Controllers
  • All data controllers and processors in Kenya processing personal data must register with the ODPC.
  • MTN Kenya's USSD AI assistant constitutes a new processing activity that must be separately registered or notified to the ODPC as an amendment to the existing registration.
  • Registration must describe: data categories, processing purposes, data subjects, retention periods, security measures, and any automated decision-making.
  • A Data Protection Officer must be designated and their details provided to the ODPC.
  • Current status: Partial — base registration exists; USSD AI amendment not yet filed.

Cross-Border Transfer Restrictions

Kenya DPA Section 49 — Restriction on Transfer Outside Kenya
  • Personal data may only be transferred outside Kenya if the recipient country ensures adequate protection equivalent to the Kenya DPA.
  • Countries with currently deemed adequate protection: none formally gazetted as of 2026 — transfers rely on contractual safeguards (Standard Contractual Clauses equivalent).
  • Shared AI infrastructure risk: If the NLU model is hosted on infrastructure shared with other MTN markets (e.g., South Africa or EU-based), Kenyan customer data processed during inference constitutes a cross-border transfer.
  • Binding Corporate Rules (BCR) equivalent or contractual clauses with ODPC notification required for group-level data sharing.
  • Transfers for AML/fraud intelligence (e.g., to INTERPOL, FATF bodies) are permitted under law enforcement exception but must be documented.
  • Current status: Gap — no cross-border transfer mechanism documented for AI inference.

Requirements Summary

Uganda DPPA 2019 — Key Obligations
  • Registration as Personal Data Collector: MTN Uganda must register with the Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO). The USSD AI assistant, as a new data collection and processing mechanism, requires notification of processing activities.
  • Lawful basis for USSD AI: Consent or performance of contract. Consent must be express and informed; constructive consent (continued use of service) is not sufficient for AI-specific processing.
  • Sensitive personal data: Financial data is classified as sensitive. Special category processing under Uganda DPPA requires explicit consent and enhanced security measures.
  • Data Protection Officer: Required for organisations processing large volumes of personal data. Must be registered with PDPO.
  • Security obligation: "Appropriate technical and organisational measures" — USSD channel's inherent lack of encryption at transport layer must be mitigated at application layer.
  • Breach notification: PDPO must be notified within 48 hours of becoming aware of a breach (stricter than POPIA's 72-hour target). Data subjects notified without undue delay.

Financial Services Compliance

AML / KYC / Transaction Limits

FSCA / SARB / FICA — South Africa

Financial Sector Conduct Authority · SA Reserve Bank · Financial Intelligence Centre Act
  • FSCA: MTN SA's Mobile Money product (MoMo) operates under a Payment System Operator (PSO) licence. USSD AI changes to transaction processing require FSCA notification and may require approval.
  • SARB NPS: National Payment System (NPS) oversight applies. AI-driven transaction routing or prioritisation must comply with NPS interoperability requirements.
  • FICA Section 21 — KYC: Customer identity verification required. AI cannot bypass KYC; intent classification that enables a transaction must confirm KYC status first. KYC data used for AI training requires specific FICA authorisation.
  • FICA Section 29 — Suspicious Activity: AI-flagged suspicious transactions must trigger a SAR (Suspicious Activity Report) to the FIC within 24 hours. AI confidence threshold for flagging must be documented and defensible.
  • Transaction limits (SARB): USSD AI must enforce: R5,000 per transaction, R25,000 per day — limits must be hardcoded in transaction layer, not softcoded in AI layer.
  • Record retention: FICA requires 5-year retention of transaction records. AI session data linked to completed transactions cannot be erased regardless of POPIA erasure requests.
  • Current status: Partial — FICA SAR integration with AI flagging not yet implemented.

CBN — Nigeria

Central Bank of Nigeria · Mobile Money Guidelines
  • CBN Mobile Money Guidelines 2021: MTN Nigeria operates under the Payment Service Bank (PSB) licence (MTN MoMo PSB). AI changes to the transaction flow require CBN prior approval.
  • KYC tiers: CBN maintains three KYC tiers with different transaction limits. AI must query KYC tier before processing: Tier 1 (₦50K/day), Tier 2 (₦200K/day), Tier 3 (₦5M/day).
  • CBN AML/CFT Regulations 2022: AI-powered transaction monitoring must flag transactions above ₦1M (single) or ₦5M (cumulative daily) for enhanced scrutiny. STR filing to NFIU within 24 hours.
  • Agent network AI: USSD AI processing agent-assisted transactions must comply with CBN Agent Banking Guidelines — agent identity verification and transaction limits apply.
  • Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS): Interbank transfers processed via AI-intent must route through NIP/NIPS infrastructure — AI cannot create alternative routing.
  • Current status: Partial — CBN approval for AI component not yet sought.

CBK — Kenya

Central Bank of Kenya · National Payment System Regulations
  • CBK NPS Regulations 2014 & amendments: Any change to the payment system technology stack (including introduction of AI for intent processing) requires CBK notification at minimum, and approval where the change affects transaction authorisation logic.
  • KYC — CBK Prudential Guidelines: USSD AI must integrate with the existing KYC database before any financial transaction. AI cannot infer KYC status; it must query authoritative records.
  • AML/CFT — POCAMLA: Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act. AI transaction monitoring must be tuned to Kenyan risk typologies. STRs filed to Kenya FIU within 3 days of suspicion arising.
  • Transaction limits (CBK): KES 300,000 per transaction, KES 500,000 per day for individual accounts — AI must enforce these limits at transaction authorisation, not at NLU layer.
  • Consumer protection: CBK Consumer Protection Guidelines require clear disclosure to customers when AI is processing their request. USSD screen must indicate "AI-assisted" where applicable.
  • Current status: Partial — CBK notification letter drafted; AI disclosure on USSD screen not yet implemented.

USSD-Specific Privacy Risks & Mitigations

Technical Risk
⚠
Fundamental USSD privacy limitation: USSD operates at the SS7 signalling layer of the GSM network. Data is transmitted in plaintext between the handset, the base station, the HLR, and the USSD gateway. There is no end-to-end encryption at the USSD protocol layer. All privacy mitigations must be implemented at the application layer above the USSD transport.
High Risk

USSD Logs at Telco Level

Telco infrastructure logs all USSD sessions including full session content. These logs contain transaction amounts, recipient MSISDNs, and AI intent classification data — all constituting personal data under applicable regulations.

Mitigation: Execute Data Processing Agreements with all telco entities limiting retention to 30 days, prohibiting secondary use, and mandating secure deletion. Classify telco as "operator" under POPIA / "data processor" under NDPR.
High Risk

Session Data Exposure

USSD sessions time out after 180 seconds of inactivity. Incomplete sessions (customer walked away mid-transaction) leave partial transaction data in the USSD gateway buffer — accessible to network administrators.

Mitigation: Implement session purge on timeout at USSD gateway level. Incomplete sessions must not persist beyond 60 seconds post-timeout. Alert on incomplete financial transactions for fraud review.
High Risk

No Encryption at USSD Layer

SS7 vulnerabilities are well-documented. State-sponsored and criminal actors can intercept USSD sessions via SS7 attacks, exposing transaction content in transit.

Mitigation: Never transmit PINs or authentication secrets via USSD session content. Use USSD only for intent capture; authenticate via separate OTP SMS channel. Implement SS7 anomaly detection on MTN USSD gateway.
Medium Risk

AI Model Data Leakage

NLU model may memorise specific customer data (MSISDN, transaction patterns) from training data. Model inversion attacks could extract personal data from the model itself.

Mitigation: Apply differential privacy during model training. Use anonymised and aggregated training data. Implement model access controls and rate limiting on inference API. Regular model audits for data leakage.
Medium Risk

SIM Swap Attack Surface

USSD AI identifies customers by MSISDN. SIM swap attacks transfer a victim's MSISDN to an attacker's SIM, giving the attacker access to USSD AI session and potentially triggering transactions.

Mitigation: Integrate with MTN's SIM swap detection API. Any MSISDN that has undergone a SIM swap within 72 hours should be flagged — require additional authentication before AI processes financial transactions.
Lower Risk

Shoulder Surfing / Physical Exposure

USSD sessions display on handset screen in plaintext. In public environments, transaction amounts and recipient details visible to bystanders.

Mitigation: Mask amounts after 3 seconds where handset OS supports it (limited USSD control). Add consumer education campaign. This risk cannot be fully mitigated at application layer — include in consumer disclosure.

AI-Specific Regulatory Considerations

NLU / Automated Decisions

Explainability of NLU Decisions

POPIA · NDPR · Kenya DPA · Uganda DPPA
  • Where the AI's intent classification affects access to financial services (loan approval, account freeze, transaction blocking), customers have the right to a meaningful explanation of the decision.
  • NLU "black box" classification must be supplemented with an explainability layer — at minimum, a rule-based override system that can generate a human-readable reason code for any classification affecting financial access.
  • Reason codes must be accessible to customer service agents who handle escalations — integrate into CRM and agent dashboard.
  • AI model documentation (model card) must be maintained describing: training data sources, accuracy by language/dialect, known limitations, and validation methodology.
  • Recommended: Implement LIME or SHAP-based explainability for loan-related NLU classifications; simpler rule-based explanation for standard transaction intents.

Bias in Intent Classification Across Languages

Anti-discrimination · Fairness Risk
  • MTN's USSD AI operates across 13 markets with diverse languages including English, French, Swahili, Yoruba, Zulu, Hausa, Luganda, Kinyarwanda, and several others.
  • NLU models trained predominantly on English data may exhibit significantly lower accuracy for non-English intents, creating discriminatory service quality. Customers expressing intents in local languages may face higher error rates, causing transaction failures disproportionately affecting specific demographic groups.
  • Regulatory risk: South Africa's Equality Act, Kenya's Constitution (Article 27), and Nigeria's NDPR all prohibit discriminatory processing. Demonstrably lower service quality for specific language groups may constitute unlawful discrimination.
  • Control: Maintain per-language accuracy metrics. Set a minimum 90% intent classification accuracy threshold per language before deployment. Monthly fairness audits comparing accuracy across language groups.

Automated Decision-Making

POPIA s71 · NDPR Art 2.5 · Kenya DPA s34 · Uganda DPPA
  • Definition: Automated decision-making occurs where the AI system makes a decision solely by automated means, without meaningful human involvement, and the decision produces legal or significant effects for the individual.
  • In-scope decisions for USSD AI: (1) Loan eligibility determination based on usage patterns, (2) Transaction blocking based on fraud score, (3) Account suspension based on AML alert.
  • Right to human review: Across all four deep-dive jurisdictions, individuals have the right to request human review of automated decisions affecting their financial access. The USSD channel must provide a clear escalation path — e.g., "Press 0 to speak to an agent about this decision."
  • Profiling: Building behavioural profiles from USSD transaction history for credit scoring constitutes profiling under NDPR and Kenya DPA. Explicit consent required; customers must be informed that their USSD behaviour affects their credit profile.
  • AI disclosure: CBK (Kenya) and FSCA (SA) guidance indicates customers must be informed when AI is processing their financial request. USSD screen tag: "[AI]" or "AI-assisted service" at session start.

Consent Ledger Data Model

Each consent record must contain:

  • → msisdn_hash — SHA-256 of MSISDN (no PII in ledger key)
  • → market_code — ISO country code (ZA, NG, KE, UG…)
  • → consent_version — version of consent notice presented
  • → consent_timestamp — UTC timestamp of opt-in
  • → consent_channel — "USSD" | "APP" | "WEB" | "AGENT"
  • → withdrawal_timestamp — UTC timestamp of opt-out (nullable)
  • → lawful_basis — "consent" | "contract" | "legal_obligation"
  • → processing_purposes[] — array of purpose codes
ℹ
Consent versioning: When the privacy notice is updated (e.g., new AI model deployed, new processing purpose added), a new consent version must be issued. Customers who consented under version N must be re-prompted on next USSD session if version N+1 introduces material changes. The consent ledger must track which version each customer consented to, and the system must not apply new processing purposes until consent to the new version is obtained.

Breach Response Workflow — USSD AI Specific

T+0 — Detection

AI anomaly detection or security monitoring identifies potential breach. USSD AI incident classified: data breach (personal data exposed), operational incident (USSD service disruption), or financial fraud event.

T+1h — Triage & Classification

Security team determines: markets affected, data categories exposed, number of data subjects affected, whether financial data (transaction amounts, account balances) was accessed.

T+4h — Internal Escalation

Information Officer / DPO notified. Legal counsel engaged. CISO and CFO briefed. Decision on whether regulatory notification threshold is met per jurisdiction.

T+48h — Uganda PDPO Notification (if applicable)

Strictest deadline. Uganda notification filed regardless of whether full investigation is complete — initial notification with follow-up permitted.

T+72h — SA / Nigeria / Kenya Regulatory Notifications

Parallel notifications to Information Regulator (SA), NITDA (Nigeria), ODPC (Kenya). CBN / FSCA financial incident reports filed where financial system integrity affected.

T+7 days — Post-Incident Report

Full root cause analysis. Remediation plan with timelines. Regulators updated. Data subject notifications sent (SMS via MSISDN + USSD notification on next session).

Audit Trail Requirements

Logging & Evidence

AI Decision Audit Log

Required for Automated Decision Accountability
  • What to log: For every NLU classification that triggers a financial action: session ID (anonymised), intent label, confidence score, fallback triggers, transaction outcome, KYC tier at time of decision, market jurisdiction.
  • What NOT to log: Raw USSD input strings, MSISDN in plaintext (use MSISDN-hash), transaction amounts after settlement.
  • Retention: AI decision audit logs retained 5 years for regulatory audit access; 2 years for operational review.
  • Access control: Read-only access for internal audit; encrypted at rest; immutable (append-only log store).
  • Format: Structured JSON with regulatory jurisdiction field to enable per-market reporting.

Transaction Audit Trail

FICA / CBN / CBK Requirements
  • Every Mobile Money transaction processed via USSD AI must have an immutable audit record: transaction ID, MSISDN-hash, amount, currency, recipient MSISDN-hash, timestamp, channel ("USSD-AI"), AI intent label, KYC tier, regulatory jurisdiction.
  • Audit records must be tamper-evident — implement WORM storage or cryptographic chaining of transaction records.
  • Regulator access: transaction audit logs must be producible to financial regulators (SARB, CBN, CBK) within 48 hours of a lawful demand.
  • Cross-border transactions must carry additional fields: originating jurisdiction, destination jurisdiction, correspondent bank route, FX rate applied.

Consent and Privacy Audit Log

All Data Protection Authorities
  • Consent ledger changes (opt-in, opt-out, version upgrade, withdrawal) must generate immutable audit events.
  • Data subject rights requests (access, erasure, portability) must be logged: request receipt timestamp, processing steps, response timestamp, outcome.
  • Regulatory inspections: all audit logs must be producible to data protection authorities within 72 hours of a lawful demand.
  • Privacy audit logs must be stored separately from transaction logs — different access controls, different retention schedules, no co-mingling of financial and privacy records.
  • Annual audit: NITDA requires annual privacy audit submission for Nigeria. Privacy audit log must support generation of the NITDA audit report format.
