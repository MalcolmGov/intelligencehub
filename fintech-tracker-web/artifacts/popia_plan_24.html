Section 1

1 Executive Summary

⚠

Regulatory Exposure — Maximum Penalty: R10,000,000 or 10% of Annual Turnover

POPIA s109 empowers the Information Regulator to impose administrative fines of up to R10 million, or 10% of annual turnover (whichever is greater), for serious infringements. Criminal sanctions include imprisonment of up to 10 years for responsible parties. A unified AI Customer Intelligence Layer that consolidates personal information across multiple systems without a POPIA-native architecture creates material regulatory and reputational risk.

🎯 Why POPIA Compliance is Non-Negotiable Here

The AI Customer Intelligence Layer creates a unified customer profile by aggregating data from 8 integrated systems — CRM, transaction ledger, communication records, fraud signals, behavioural analytics, support tickets, marketing engagement, and external credit data. This aggregation constitutes large-scale automated processing of personal information under POPIA, triggering heightened obligations including DPIA requirements, explicit consent management, and mandatory breach notification. As an FSP operating under FSCA, non-compliance compounds exposure under both POPIA and sector-specific prudential requirements.

🏗 POPIA-Native by Design

This compliance plan mandates that POPIA requirements are embedded into the system architecture from the first line of code — not retrospectively bolted on. Privacy by Design (PbD) and Privacy by Default are foundational architectural principles. Every data element ingested, transformed, or generated by the AI layer must have a documented lawful processing basis, an explicit retention period, field-level access controls, and a deletion propagation path. Consent management and right-to-erasure workflows are first-class system components, not edge cases.

📋 Scope

All personal information processed by UC24, including data ingested from 8 source systems, AI-derived scores and predictions, consent records, audit logs, and breach documentation. Covers all data subjects whose information is held — customers, prospects, and guarantors.

👤 Responsible Party

The organisation (FinTech entity) is the Responsible Party under POPIA s1. A Data Protection Officer (DPO) must be designated prior to go-live. Malcolm Govender (Head of AI & Engineering) is the technical POPIA lead for UC24.

📅 Compliance Target

Full POPIA compliance must be demonstrated prior to production launch. The Information Regulator's Enforcement Date was 1 July 2021 — the organisation is already subject to full enforcement. No grace period applies.

Section 3

3 Consent Management Design

⚡

Consent Must Be Freely Given, Specific, Informed, and Unambiguous

POPIA s11(1)(a) requires consent to be a voluntary, specific, and informed expression of will. Pre-ticked boxes, bundled consent, and implicit consent do not satisfy this standard. Where consent is the lawful basis for any UC24 processing, it must be granular, per-purpose, and withdrawable at any time with equal ease.

📝 Consent Capture Requirements

Every consent event must record the following attributes as an immutable, timestamped record:

Data subject identifier (hashed)
Purpose code(s) consented to — granular, per-purpose
Timestamp (UTC, millisecond precision)
Channel (web, mobile, branch, IVR, agent-assisted)
Consent version / privacy notice version at time of capture
IP address / device fingerprint (where applicable)
Agent ID (for agent-assisted consent captures)
Exact text of consent statement presented to data subject

🔒 Consent Storage — Immutable Ledger

Consent records are stored in a tamper-evident, write-once ledger separate from operational databases. Records must be retained indefinitely as evidence of lawful processing basis (see Section 5). Each record is cryptographically signed. Consent history (all versions, withdrawals, re-consents) is preserved in full — no overwriting. The ledger must be queryable in real-time by the system to gate processing decisions at runtime.

🔄 Consent Withdrawal — Propagation Workflow

When a data subject withdraws consent the system must: (1) Record withdrawal in the consent ledger immediately with timestamp and channel; (2) Halt all consent-dependent processing within 1 hour of receipt; (3) Propagate the withdrawal event via the message bus to all 8 integrated systems, each of which must return a delivery acknowledgement; (4) Confirm withdrawal to the data subject in writing via their preferred channel. Full propagation SLA: 24 hours across all 8 systems. Withdrawal must be as easy as the original consent — accessible from the self-service portal, email, or branch without friction or dark patterns.

🔄 Re-Consent Triggers

Fresh, explicit consent must be obtained when any of the following occur:

Processing purpose changes or materially expands
New third-party data sharing arrangements introduced
Privacy notice materially updated
AI model uses data in a new way (e.g., profiling for credit decisions)
Periodic consent refresh — rolling 24-month cycle for inactive data subjects
Cross-border transfer destination changes

Consent UI/UX: Separate consent per purpose. Plain language (Grade 8 reading level). Available in all supported languages. No bundled consent.

Section 4

4 Right-to-Erasure Implementation

⏱

POPIA s24 — 30-Day Response Obligation

Upon receipt of a valid erasure request, the Responsible Party must respond within 30 days (s24). Erasure must be comprehensive — covering all systems where the personal information resides. Failure to delete from any integrated system constitutes ongoing non-compliance.

1

Request Intake — Multi-Channel

Erasure requests accepted via: (1) Self-service DSR portal (authenticated session), (2) Email to the designated POPIA address (dpo@[company].co.za), (3) Branch / telephonic request — agent captures and escalates. All requests assigned a unique reference number and logged in the DSR management system within 1 business day of receipt. Acknowledgement sent to data subject confirming receipt and 30-day SLA.

Day 0 — Request Received
2

Identity Verification Workflow

Before any erasure is executed, the data subject's identity must be verified to prevent fraudulent erasure of a third party's data. Verification is risk-proportionate: (a) Portal — two-factor authentication via registered device and OTP; (b) Email — knowledge-based verification (last 4 digits of ID number, account number, registered address); (c) Branch — FICA-grade identity document verification in person. Verification outcome documented and attached to the DSR record.

Days 1–3 — Identity Verification
3

Retention Exception Assessment

Prior to erasure, the system checks whether a legal hold, regulatory retention requirement, or active legal proceeding prevents full erasure. Exceptions with documented legal basis: (a) FICA — 5-year retention of transaction records; (b) Active litigation — legal hold flag applied; (c) Regulatory investigation — Information Regulator or FSCA instruction; (d) Fraud investigation — 7-year retention of fraud signals. Where exceptions apply, the data subject is notified in writing of what was retained and the specific legal basis.

Days 3–7 — Exception Review
4

Pseudonymisation vs. Hard Delete Decision

Hard delete is the default where no retention exception applies. Pseudonymisation may be used only where deletion would destroy data required for legal or regulatory compliance — the pseudonymised data must be de-linked from all identifiers, technically irreversible without the separated key, and inaccessible to operational systems. See the decision tree below.

5

Erasure Propagation — All 8 Integrated Systems

Erasure command issued via the central event bus to all integrated systems in sequence: (1) AI Unified Profile — primary record deleted / pseudonymised; (2) CRM — customer record purged; (3) Transaction Ledger — subject identifiers removed (amounts retained for FICA compliance); (4) Communication Records — interaction history purged; (5) Fraud Signal Store — identifiers pseudonymised, values retained 7 years; (6) Behavioural Analytics — event stream purged; (7) Support Ticket System — PII fields overwritten; (8) Marketing Engagement Platform — contact record deleted and suppression list updated. Each system returns a deletion acknowledgement with timestamp.

Days 7–25 — Propagation Across All 8 Systems
6

Confirmation to Data Subject

Written confirmation sent to the data subject within 30 days of original request, documenting: what was deleted, which systems were cleared, what (if anything) was retained and why, and the reference number for the erasure event. Confirmation must be sent via the same channel as the original request unless the data subject specifies otherwise.

Day ≤30 — Written Confirmation
7

Audit Trail — Permanent Record of Erasure

An immutable audit record of every erasure event is maintained indefinitely: data subject reference (pseudonymised), date of request, date of erasure, systems cleared, fields deleted vs. retained, legal basis for any retention, verifying officer, and method of confirmation to data subject. This audit trail contains no personal information about the data subject beyond a pseudonymised reference.

Section 6

6 PII Classification Framework

🔴

Special Personal Information (s26) — Prohibited Processing Without Explicit Authorisation

POPIA s26 prohibits processing of special personal information (SPI) except in narrowly defined circumstances. SPI includes religious or philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion, health or sex life, biometric information, and criminal behaviour. The AI Customer Intelligence Layer must not ingest, derive, or infer SPI without a documented s27 or s28 exception. AI models must be audited for SPI proxy risk — features that correlate with SPI categories even without directly processing them. Violation constitutes a criminal offence.

Section 7

7 Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)

📊

DPIA Required Before Build Commences

The AI Customer Intelligence Layer involves large-scale automated processing, profiling, and aggregation of personal information from multiple sources — all high-risk indicators requiring a DPIA. The DPIA must be completed, reviewed by the DPO, and signed off before system build commences. It is a living document — reassessed at each major architecture change or new data source onboarding.

High Risk

Aggregation Risk — Unified Profile

Combining data from 8 systems creates a comprehensive profile far more sensitive than any individual source system. Even lawfully collected data, when aggregated, may enable inferences about SPI categories (e.g., spending patterns inferring health conditions, location history inferring religious attendance).

Mitigation: Purpose limitation enforced at the query layer. Profile completeness threshold — block field combinations beyond the approved set. Algorithmic audit for SPI proxy risk in model features. Minimum data principle enforced at API level.
High Risk

Re-identification Risk

Anonymised or pseudonymised data used in model training or analytics may be re-identified when combined with external datasets (quasi-identifier attack). This is an active threat in South Africa's relatively small, structured fintech customer base.

Mitigation: k-anonymity ≥ 5 enforced on all analytics exports. Differential privacy techniques applied to aggregate queries. External data join controls — prohibited without DPIA amendment and DPO approval.
High Risk

Automated Decision-Making

AI-generated credit risk scores and fraud signals may inform decisions with significant legal or financial effect on data subjects (credit decline, account restriction). POPIA s71 grants data subjects the right to challenge such decisions and request human review.

Mitigation: Explainability layer on all decision-affecting models (SHAP/LIME). Human-in-the-loop mandatory for adverse credit decisions. Automated decision register maintained. s71 request workflow implemented in the DSR portal.
Medium Risk

Operator / Third-Party Risk

Cloud infrastructure, ML platform providers, and integrated SaaS vendors process personal information as operators under s21. Any breach or non-compliance by an operator creates direct liability for the Responsible Party.

Mitigation: Written operator agreements with POPIA-compliant clauses mandatory before any data sharing. Annual operator compliance audits. Data processing addendum (DPA) required. Cross-border adequacy assessment for all non-SA cloud regions.
Medium Risk

Model Drift & Data Quality Degradation

Stale or inaccurate data used in AI models violates the Information Quality condition (s16) and may result in unfair or discriminatory outcomes, creating both POPIA and Treating Customers Fairly (TCF) exposure.

Mitigation: Automated data quality monitoring with alerting thresholds. Model drift detection dashboard. Data refresh SLAs per category enforced. Bias monitoring in model outputs. Scheduled model revalidation every 6 months.
Residual Risk

Residual Risk After Mitigation

After all identified mitigations are implemented, residual risk is assessed as low-medium. No processing activity presents an unacceptable residual risk that would require prior consultation with the Information Regulator before commencing processing.

Assessment: Residual risk accepted by DPO and CISO. Annual DPIA refresh mandated. Incident monitoring as leading indicator. Next DPIA review: prior to any major feature extension or new data source onboarding.
Section 8

8 Breach Notification Plan

🚨

POPIA s22 — Mandatory Notification to Information Regulator & Data Subjects

Upon becoming aware of a breach, the Responsible Party must notify the Information Regulator and affected data subjects as soon as reasonably possible. Industry best practice targets 72 hours for Regulator notification. Failure to notify is itself a criminal offence under s109. The breach notification plan must be tested annually via tabletop exercise.

1

Detection

Breach detected via: automated security monitoring (SIEM alerts), anomaly detection on data access patterns, third-party notification (operator or vendor), or data subject report. All potential breach signals escalated to the Security Operations Centre (SOC) immediately. SOC initiates triage within 1 hour of alert. All security events that could constitute a breach are treated as confirmed until definitively ruled out.

Hour 0 — Detection & Triage
2

Internal Escalation & Containment

Incident Commander designated. Containment measures activated (isolate affected systems, revoke compromised credentials, preserve forensic evidence). CISO, DPO, Legal, and CEO notified within 4 hours of confirmed breach. Severity classification applied: (a) Critical — SPI or financial data exposed, large scale; (b) High — Standard PII, limited scope; (c) Medium — pseudonymised or encrypted data only. Breach assessment completed: scope, nature of data, number of data subjects affected, likely harm.

Hours 1–12 — Escalation & Assessment
3

Information Regulator Notification — 72-Hour Target

Notification to the Information Regulator submitted within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach (internal target: 48 hours). Notification must include: nature of the breach, categories and approximate number of data subjects affected, categories and approximate number of records affected, likely consequences, and measures taken or proposed. DPO is the responsible officer for all Regulator communications. Notification submitted via the Information Regulator's prescribed form.

Hour ≤72 — Information Regulator Notification
4

Data Subject Notification

Affected data subjects notified as soon as reasonably practicable after Regulator notification. Notification via registered email, SMS, and in-app alert. Notification must include: nature of the breach in plain language, contact details of the DPO, recommended protective steps the data subject should take, and whether a complaint has been lodged with the Information Regulator. Notification exempted only where it would impede a criminal investigation (law enforcement written confirmation required).

ASAP after Regulator — Data Subject Notification
5

Post-Incident Review & Breach Register

Formal post-incident review within 14 days of containment. Root cause analysis documented, remediation actions assigned with owners and deadlines, DPIA amended where required. Breach register updated with full incident record and retained indefinitely as evidence of s22 compliance. Annual tabletop exercise to test the full notification workflow including escalation contacts and Regulator communication channel.
